# Hearth Pass — 20260904-181836

## Idea

Some Dominion households deliberately exile one beloved relative from every visible part of family life so that somebody remains administratively clean enough to take the household in when a work flag reaches its housing and clinic routes. The trick survives because the evidentiary firewall protecting GeneSys dynasties from shared liability also protects a genuinely separate address; using it buys one intake interval, burns the separation, and forces the whole block to begin cultivating the next absence.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

- **Benefits:** a flagged household gains one clean housing or clinic intake before association review catches up; children, older relatives, disabled people, and patients with route-bound care keep continuity; neighbors gain a repeatable way to help without founding an institution the Dominion can seize.
- **Harmed:** the deliberately estranged relative loses ordinary presence and recognized authority; people with portable work, few registered dependants, or weak standing are pressured into becoming the absence; genuine estrangement and abuse become harder for neighbors and clerks to distinguish from protective performance.
- **Costs:** no shared address, payment, emergency authority, logged visit, or easy public mourning; neighbor relays absorb errands and secrecy; activating the clean address consumes it, and a model that joins the relay can spread the original flag into several households.

## Rejected first ideas

- A second shift-door medicine, food, and child-handover table — already owned by the earlier Hearth pass and its Pallas care table.
- A formal neighborhood mutual-aid office — too legible, too easily captured, and a safe rival institution where the mandate asks for something small and functioning.
- Contract adoption or broad chosen-family recognition — the predictable extension of co-location appeals, without a new private cost or a reason the Dominion itself must leave the seam open.
