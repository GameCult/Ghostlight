# Veil Pass — 20260904-195734

## Idea

NeuroSyn's welfare pruning does not delete anomalous distress; it relabels the signal as acceptable adaptation variance and feeds it into a cross-site baseline called the **Accrued Normal**, so every concealed injury teaches other facilities not to see the next one. Insurers and certifiers depend on the resulting low-false-alarm models for mixed engineered bodies, which means exposing the lineage can invalidate safety findings, freeze work, and withdraw the very interface support keeping captives and workers alive.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

- **Benefits:** NeuroSyn turns client-cleared anomalies into a more portable model and preserves subscription revenue; employers lose fewer hours to welfare stops; insurers and procurement offices receive stable false-alarm rates and a cheap, current configuration finding.
- **Harmed:** captive uplifts, biodrones, and workers with unfamiliar or recently altered bodies are measured against harm suffered by people like them and administratively declared tolerable. Auditors and technical staff are also kept inside narrow truths that prevent any one honest actor from seeing the causal join.
- **Costs:** each suppressed event shifts the classification boundary for later sites. Proving contamination can suspend certification, raise coverage, revoke hazard credentials, and interrupt SkillPacks or affect support; preserving the proof requires body-owned baselines, model-release evidence, material-care receipts, distributed custody, time, and a survivable exit plan.

## Rejected first ideas

- A protected raw-telemetry or clinical-session buffer — already occupied by the earlier Veil pass; renaming its privacy seam would be repetition with better stationery.
- A containment lead simply deletes distress logs before inspection — the fixture already supplies that obvious act, and deletion alone creates no new world pressure.
- A welfare-data black market selling captives' neural records — NeuroSyn already monetizes telemetry; adding a criminal buyer would only widen an existing transaction.
- A rule transferring corporate liability onto the first employee who joins the hidden records — surprising, but it required a bespoke legal trap instead of following the vault's existing telemetry and certification machinery.
- Retrospectively removing dead, escaped, or reclassified subjects from a welfare denominator — plausible statistical fraud, but too easy and too local to change what stories the world can hold.
