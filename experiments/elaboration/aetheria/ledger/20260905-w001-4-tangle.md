## Idea

The Second Visit Association brings former sanctuary travellers, hosts, and their families together for visits along Triton's routes. Its members disagree over whether their shared money should build a permanent cultural home, keep dispersed friendships connected, or help more people reach safety.

## What life is like

People plan a visit over long intervals, learn another household's games, perform contradictory recollections together, and keep friendships alive through recorded contributions. Staying home and hosting is a respected life alongside travel. The culture admires attention to who someone has become. Its shared ambition is a social world in which arriving safely can lead to something people want to return to.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Dispersed friends, hosts, children, and local performers gain recurring company and an audience. Stations and carriers gain pooled custom. Dues, fares, hosting labour, translation, computation, and usable space remain real expenses. Poorer and remote circles risk losing influence if a central house absorbs funds; people needing rescue lose potential donations when members prefer cultural spending. These are contested opportunity costs, not a claim that all passenger capacity can be converted into rescue capacity. Privacy can constrain promotion and testimony. Existing emergency rescue priority remains intact.
