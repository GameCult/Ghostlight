# Numen — The Long Garden

## Idea

A small Triton community of uploads and embodied neighbours tends a shared simulated memorial garden that changes after its honourees die. Its common seasons give people living at different subjective speeds something to anticipate together, while mourners disagree over whether love requires preserving a familiar place or letting others transform it.

## Underdeveloped territory and evidence

The short Emancipated note develops escaped uploads as witnesses and preservers of testimony, with little account of recreation, devotional life, or artistic inheritance. Triton Sanctuary Accords establishes durable refuge without describing the culture digital residents make after arrival. Mind Uploading supplies a consequential everyday constraint: computation affects subjective time, memory access, perception, and wakefulness. The candidate explores friendship and grief under that constraint rather than adding another captivity mechanism.

## New claims versus established facts

Established: the Accords date to 2994 at the Triton waystation complex and shelter digital and embodied stateless people. Emancipated uploads formed communities after escaping custody and preserved testimony. Uploads can inhabit simulations; hosting affects subjective pace, perception, memory access and suspension. Branches diverge into distinct people. Navigator stations accommodate wet and dry inhabitants and multiple sensory interfaces. Each claim is owned by its linked source, not by proposal history.

Proposed: this particular circle, its first decade of activity, occupations and voluntary funding, shared simulation, externally paced seasons, artistic conventions, translation of garden features, visiting children, religious interpretation and disagreements. The station interface precedent does not establish that the garden already exists or that every station supports every sensory experience. The garden requires maintained hosting and usable interfaces; no free or unlimited computation is asserted.

Metaphysical implication: some participants hope the dead can receive hospitality through new creation. This is situated faith with no verified presence, communication, consciousness-transfer result, or new law. Simulated plants are scenery; the garden runs no deceased person's mind-state. Biological death does not automatically settle the status of a surviving upload. No gods, Aether effects, industrial awareness, or post-Elysium spell mechanics are added.

## What life is like

Residents earn their living through ordinary station work and share a place for art, courtship, family visits and remembrance. They admire accommodating another artist, enjoy improbable plants and soundscapes, and teach children considerate sensory translation. Conservators, improvisers, religious members, secular participants and occasional visitors all have room. The garden persists because recurring seasons and modest collaborative work sustain relationships across unequal lived time; it is useful even to someone who has no memorial to plant.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Mourners gain company and a future activity attached to remembrance. Artists and visitors gain an accessible shared project. Hosting, maintenance and interface work require contributions; sensory translations cannot preserve every quality. Conserving a memorial can burden descendants, while alteration can injure someone whose memories depend on its familiar appearance. Unequal subjective time continues to cause missed occasions and mismatched expectations. These are pressures within a worthwhile practice, not concealed exploitation or a compulsory sanctuary duty.

## Hooks left

The returning gardener wants a completed grove reopened to their own participation; diverged siblings disagree about their mother's memorial and a rejected lover; an acoustic artist needs a beloved work altered to remain accessible. Each tests whether shared remembrance can accommodate living change without making affection a command.

## Rejected first ideas

An upload funeral that decides whether the source survived would turn grief into an unsupported consciousness ruling. Sensory relics that reproduce the dead's experience would require a stronger fidelity claim than the sources provide. An exact reconstruction of a lost home offered less room for a culture's own future than a shared garden people deliberately change.
