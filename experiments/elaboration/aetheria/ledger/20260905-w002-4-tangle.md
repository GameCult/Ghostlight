## What life is like

People service passing ships and follow returning scientific observations as a local entertainment. Precision, daring predictions, and public rivalry earn admiration; belonging also leaves room for mechanics, music enthusiasts, racers, and people bored by the entire subject. Science is a pleasure with an audience before it becomes the expedition's political cause. Established families and would-be settlers can want different futures without either being a hidden villain.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Public-science supporters gain a chance to choose questions and publish observations. Settlement supporters seek space and independent livelihoods; the carrier seeks dependable future stops. Paid workshop labor gains work. Financing through local savings and workshop time limits the scale without a new tax or mandatory household sacrifice. Expensive wet accommodation creates a concrete risk that crew selection will exclude the very residents whose region is sponsoring the journey. Rescue commitments, reserves, and technical readiness remain outside the clubs' power to wish away.

The political leverage is an open offer of real help: accepting it strengthens one local coalition. No contract trap or concealed exploitation is needed. Whether the proposed settlement site is useful remains genuinely unknown.
