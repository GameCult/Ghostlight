# Ember Pass — 20260904-195037

## Idea

Late-Sol creditors can keep a failing habitat out of salvage only by leaving one legally recognized person aboard: a Last Witness whose live replies make the asset count as occupied even after every useful worker and rescue seat has gone. Because departure converts the habitat into abandonable collateral and accelerates every attached debt, firms make a worker’s passage, family housing, and wages conditional on remaining inside until a replacement arrives—turning administrative personhood into literal jurisdictional ballast.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

- **Benefits:** creditors preserve continuous-possession claims through evacuation; insurers, receivers, ports, clearing houses, and salvage buyers receive a legible trigger instead of an open jurisdictional fight. A witness sometimes gains hazard pay, departure priority, and leverage from being able to destroy more portfolio value by leaving than their labor could ever earn.
- **Harmed:** low-status people whose recognized personhood makes them eligible for exposure; their families and dependants, whose housing, clinic access, wages, and passage secure the witness’s compliance; uplifts who can be denied recognition when they need standing and granted it when a creditor needs a body; rescue crews forced to choose between a life and the claim protecting everyone else’s destination.
- **Costs:** the holder must keep minimal air, power, water, communications, and relief capacity alive in an otherwise stripped asset. A presence lapse accelerates debt and reprices multiple dependent contracts, while second-chair circles spend travel credit, equipment, hazard credentials, clean records, and another person’s time under the clock to make one departure possible.

## Hooks left

- If a Last Witness is reclassified as property or a nonperson during the bond, does the asset become vacant while they are still alive inside it?
- When rescue creates a presence lapse, who caused the default: the crew that removed the witness or the creditor that failed to provide the promised replacement?
- Does a dreamer repository hold millions of occupants, or none at all, when no awake rights-bearing person can answer the second call?

## Rejected first ideas

- A proprietary nutrient-culture replenishment default — explicitly left by the earlier Ember pass and only the safe food-side neighbor of its convoy cascade.
- A hatch deliberately kept uncertified because its known second-kick fault is cheaper than a shutdown order — vivid fixture material, but not a new setting concept.
- Repositories selling brief awakenings as labor to pay their own cooling debt — a predictable extension of the existing dreamer-city machinery, and too close to detailing an owner that already exists.
- A generic evacuation insurance exclusion — already covered in spirit by the swarm’s default, custody, and covenant work; it needed the stranger inversion where recognized occupancy prevents departure.
