## What life is like

Workers earn a second reputation through comic timing, scenic invention, or discerning attendance. The familiar play rewards repeated visits because everyone anticipates its disaster and watches the performer delay it. An ambitious artist can sincerely discover a quieter taste while still depending on the old act's appeal. Pell's concern is being made ridiculous by his own partner, as well as preserving bookings; Sera wants room to change without pretending she always disliked the work that made her known.

Tonal blend: affectionate popular-theatre observation with social embarrassment and a little domestic friction. The principal choice is how to perform together now, not whether to leave a community or preserve its history.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Audiences gain inexpensive recurring entertainment; performers and prop makers gain limited income and standing beyond work. Minimal staging offers a different route to admiration without a large prop collection. Established acts can lose status before losing customers, and performers with different tastes can damage each other's work. Room hire, rehearsal time, equipment upkeep, and comfortable movement constrain the activity. These costs do not conceal a new contract trap or turn enjoyment into propaganda.
