## What life is like

Members follow favourite performers, rehearse with friends, exchange news between visits, teach sound editing, and become curious about a person they thought they already knew. Some want polished comedy, some unrehearsed discovery, and some the pleasure of a familiar act. Public recognition can help someone become a teacher or political supporter without requiring them to surrender the story of their arrival. The same recognition can keep an old role attached to them.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Former arrivals gain collaborators and local standing; hosts gain relationships that can grow beyond hospitality; audiences gain entertainment and familiarity across station constituencies. Strong partnerships can introduce competing political concerns to people who would otherwise hear only one side. Quieter partners may be overshadowed, and popularity can freeze a person into an outdated role. Campaign attention risks making an enjoyable performance feel tactical. Work schedules, access equipment, translation, maintained computing, food, and fares constrain participation. A modest local gathering is repeatable; frequent distant travel is not promised.
