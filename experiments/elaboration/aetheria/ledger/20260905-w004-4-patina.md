## Idea

At a small post-Elysium repair landing, people make folding picture books that accompany an ordinary walk past views of unfamiliar spatial geometry. Their growing popularity gives artists a choice between showing a destination's surprise in advance and composing the experience of discovering it on foot.

## What life is like

Tonal choice: patient visual wonder, sociable craft, and light observational humor. Repairs create waiting time and returning acquaintances. Locals draw, teach, print outlines, sell refreshments, revisit viewpoints, and develop reputations through compositions that customers actually use. Children can enjoy a strange view without becoming prodigies. Esra wants to make the walk widely desirable; Nalin enjoys pacing someone's first encounter. Other makers have interests in precision, reflections, accessible viewpoints, and unfinished work spanning return visits.

The newcomer's whole-note reread retained explicit explanations of the folded book, the stable passage, and the viewpoint sequence. The cultural choice concerns artistic experience and ambition, rather than a dispute that can be solved by one reasonable consent form.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Residents and passing crews gain an inexpensive destination, shared knowledge of views, and reasons to look closely. Part-time makers gain sales and teaching work; frequent customers accumulate familiar pleasures across work journeys. No victim is necessary to sustain the custom. Costs are materials, cleaning, ordinary enclosure maintenance, makers' time, and the opportunity cost of choosing local teaching or wider exhibition. A viewpoint chosen without regard for seated visitors wastes those customers' money and experience. The free walk relies on a gallery already maintained as part of the landing; the proposal does not require souvenir income to fund an entire settlement's life support.
