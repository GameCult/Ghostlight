## What life is like

Warm amateur seriousness: neighbors recognize an unfinished piece, friends play passages its composer cannot yet manage, and listeners anticipate another player's answer. Teaching and receiving a good variation confer local prestige. Some residents enjoy recordings or merely listening. Rusk seeks collaborators and new compositions; Pell wants instruments they have made to be played in distant communities. Their shared pleasure survives their disagreement.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Players gain craft, friendship, an audience, and ambitions beyond survival work. Distant learners gain music they can alter and techniques they can reuse. No deliberate victim is necessary. Special material, tuning labor, cargo space, and evenings are finite; choosing production delays new rehearsals, while choosing adaptation postpones Pell's material craft ambition. The existing workshop and distributed network keep the practice supportable without a new office or service apparatus.
