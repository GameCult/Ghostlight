## What life is like

Warm domestic texture with mild horticultural vanity. People work, raise children, learn tastes, host relatives, sing, cook, court one another, and want different futures for a small shared place. A resident can love cultivation and dislike communal dining. A young person can aspire to improve the orchard instead of escaping it, and an older person can favor experimentation instead of preservation.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Residents gain shared leisure, teaching, good fruit, and a place where several kinds of competence are valued. Occasional visitors help finance amenities. The practice uses growing space, energy, money, and recurring work that could serve another crop or purpose; established enthusiasts can crowd out newcomers' experiments. No hidden harm is required. Replacing a tree sacrifices reliable fruit and an attachment; keeping it can indefinitely postpone another grower's ambition. Hospitality also costs cooks and hosts effort, even when guests are welcome.
