---
title: The Ruskettle Country
description: An orcish wool country deciding who will own the town it wants to build.
---

*Canon candidate — proposed regional culture.*

In the Ruskettle country, a good wool blanket is wide enough for someone to join you. People bring them onto the low walls outside their houses after supper, shuffle sideways for a neighbour, and complain when a newly courting couple occupies an entire one. Bright borders show where a weaver learned; the middle is usually plain, because somebody has to look at it every evening.

These predominantly [[Orcs|orcish]] communities occupy a belt of grassy hills around a broad river valley. Their sheep spend summer on the heights and winter near the villages. Households sell wool, cheese and finished cloth, grow oats and roots, and buy extra grain from neighbouring [[Halflings|halfling farming settlements]]. Several generations of shared grazing, marriage and cloth trading have made Ruskettle a recognizable country across clan boundaries. A traveller identifies home by the hills first, then by kin.

Children learn to mend before they receive a blanket of their own. They also learn the shepherds' long calling songs, whose answering verses let separated walkers enjoy an argument across a hillside. Weavers shorten the same tunes to fit a room. People admire someone who can keep company through dull work without demanding everyone's attention. A quiet cheesemaker can be much sought after.

Many families follow their flocks each summer; others hire shepherds and stay beside their looms. The riverside village of Kettleford has dyers, carters, a mill and permanent shops. Its autumn fair brings the scattered households together for weeks. Cousins share rented rooms, marriages acquire witnesses who would otherwise be days apart, and old friends resume games left unfinished the previous year. Leaving has become harder for people whose closest company lives elsewhere in the hills.

Across these differences, a popular ambition has taken hold: make Kettleford a proper town, with enough workshops and customers to sell finished Ruskettle cloth throughout the year. At present, merchants carry much of the fleece to neighbouring [[Humans|human towns]], where its later sale supports other people's dyers, innkeepers and apprentices. Ruskettle families want those livelihoods close enough for their children to choose them without leaving the country.

The argument concerns the houses.

The largest flock-owning clans offer to build whole streets on their winter meadows. They would keep the buildings and place relatives in them at modest rents. Their elders expect prosperous town households to help with lambing and shelter country cousins during the fair. To families who have endured ruinous winters together, this seems a sensible extension of kinship. It also gives those elders a lasting say over who can afford to stay.

Dyers and hired shepherds have begun pooling savings to buy a smaller riverside site outright. Their supporters call themselves the Own-Hearth gathering. They want houses that inhabitants can keep after a quarrel with their clan. Several still plan to spend summer in the hills. For them, a town home would make seasonal work easier, while giving a married couple somewhere neither family could lend to visiting cousins over their objections.

Urvek, a widowed flock owner, backs the clan streets and has already promised timber. His daughter Jadra keeps the Own-Hearth savings in her dye shop. She expects her father to come and live with her when the hills grow difficult; he cannot understand why she wants to purchase what he is offering her. She remembers the aunt who offered him advice about raising daughters whenever his roof needed repairs.

Outside allies complicate the choice. Halfling grain sellers favour more permanent customers and will extend seasonal credit to either camp. A neighbouring human cloth merchants' league offers the clans several years of reliable fleece purchases. That would pay for roofs quickly, but tie up wool the proposed town workshops need. Local wool dealers support the offer; dyers have persuaded some small flock owners that better cloth prices justify waiting. Clan loyalties run through both groups.

Three matters remain open:

- Jadra has found an affordable site, but its seller uses the meadow to winter the sheep of two households too poor to buy fodder. Those households want places in the new town and an answer about their flocks.
- Urvek has invited the merchants' league to the autumn fair. His sister intends to lay locally finished cloth across the table before anyone signs.
- Two young shepherds have ordered one double-width blanket and reserved separate rooms in the proposed street. Their families have only been told about the blanket.
