# The Near Flights

*Canon candidate. Near Flights and Folded Slope are provisional descriptive names.*

At Folded Slope, a small chain of nesting ledges on the [[World/Sa'ueia Continent|Sa'ueia continent]] before imperial discovery, threadwings come home to fly very short distances together. Between a sheltered ledge and the next warm branch, a companion makes a familiar turn slowly enough to be followed. Sometimes the follower lands early. The other circles back. Being someone worth coming home to includes knowing when to turn back.

[[Biosphere/Couriers and Pollinators/Threadwing Couriers|Threadwings]] sense pressure, heat, chemicals, and memory residues through ribbonlike vanes that fray and regrow. This community has built a custom around a proposed consequence of that regrowth: a familiar current can feel different through changed ribbons. A courier can remember a route perfectly and still need practice judging a tight landing. The near flights give that practice a social place. They also give companions an excuse to spend time together without carrying anything anywhere.

## Learning Another Body's Pace

Pairs often fly together, but the custom also joins nest neighbors, old rivals, and adults who have stopped sharing a nest. One threadwing learns how much room another currently needs. Care means choosing a broad turn, leaving an easy landing open, and approaching for parasite preening only after the other settles nearby and exposes its vanes. A return from a long route begins with observation; yesterday's confident flyer may want an unusually spacious approach today.

Young flyers learn these habits alongside flight itself. An adult crosses a gentle plume, waits on a low branch, then lets the learner choose whether to follow. Later they exchange positions. The admired teacher can follow an awkward beginner without taking over the route. Experienced couriers sometimes find this more difficult than crossing the slope in poor weather.

The same short course supports a game: two flyers repeat a sequence of turns, each adding one small variation when taking the lead. The pleasure lies in finding a movement the other can answer. Close companions develop sequences outsiders cannot immediately follow. These are learned bodily habits, not memories copied whole between minds. A solitary threadwing can practice the course too, and some prefer that before inviting anyone to watch.

## A Neighborhood That Pays for Time

The ledges lie among [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|lantern trees]], whose shelter and nectar carry obligations. Folded Slope's residents pollinate the grove and carry news along short local routes. They negotiate access to several sheltered flight spaces beside their roosts. Keeping those spaces available costs feeding opportunities and useful traffic capacity; a tree with little sugar cannot support unlimited leisure.

Reliable local service makes the arrangement worth maintaining. Threadwings gain a place to regain confidence and sustain attachments. The trees retain pollinators familiar with their signals. Neither needs to understand the other's entire experience for the relationship to last. The threadwings' [[Ecology/Mutable Memory Endosymbiosis|internal memory partners]] still impose demands of their own; affection does not suspend cargo care or colony obligations.

Residents differ over how much of life should fit within this small circuit. Some take pride in knowing every landing and every neighbor's approach. Others bring home difficult movements learned far away and hope to leave again with a companion beside them. Long-distance skill attracts admiration, but so does the quiet competence of a resident whose nearest branch is always easy to reach.

The hardest arguments concern a good offer elsewhere. A courier may want an unfamiliar route while its mate has only just become comfortable flying together again. Staying can preserve something both love; leaving can be a wanted enlargement of life. Some pairs travel separately and renew their shared sequence on return. Others choose shorter journeys. No biological rule promises that either choice will save the attachment.

## Unresolved Hooks

- Two former nestmates still fly their old sequence together. One has a new companion who wants to learn it; the other keeps changing the final turn.
- A poor nectar season leaves room for only one sheltered course. Residents disagree between preserving the easiest landings and keeping the wider circuit that makes practice enjoyable for confident flyers.
- A returning courier offers to teach a spectacular new movement. Its usual partner asks for their slow old flight first, with departure already approaching.
