---
title: Understep House Plays
description: A goblin neighbourhood where playing your family well can become a difficult kind of affection.
---

In Understep, a goblin child can bring a room to silence by setting down a cup exactly as her father does. Everyone knows the argument that follows that particular cup. Tonight she gives him a different argument, about whether a borrowed stool has become a relative through length of residence. Her father laughs first. He also checks where his stool has gone.

Understep occupies a cluster of inhabited chambers beside the fungal beds of an active dungeon. Its [[Goblins|goblin]] households cultivate food, sort and move recovered material, and repair passages. Between those jobs, people put on short plays in one another's homes. A cleared floor, two lamps, and somewhere to sit are usually enough. The best-known characters are living neighbours, played with recognisable gestures and entirely invented predicaments.

People call these house plays. A good performer catches how someone wants to appear: the sister who makes every request sound like a favour she is granting, the lover who rehearses casual entrances, the quiet neighbour whose politeness becomes more elaborate as his patience runs out. Mere repetition wears thin. Audiences admire the actor who can put a familiar person into an impossible situation and make their next move feel inevitable.

Children begin with possessions: a bowl, a coat, the stretch of bedding their siblings keep invading. Adults enjoy stranger premises. In one favourite, a household adopts a lost chair and cannot agree which parent it resembles. Another gives a fungus grower the romantic attentions of every cap in her beds. [[Monsters|Dungeon creatures]] appear as cloth shapes or actors beneath blankets; a stage spider may dispute the rent without anyone believing its wild counterpart would do so. Experienced hosts keep performances away from occupied passages and working beds. Tonight's audience still needs somewhere safe to sleep.

There is no expectation that every household performs. Some host, some lend lamps, and some shut their doors when the benches start moving. Understep has excellent repairers whom nobody wishes to hear deliver a second line. Neighbours who need quiet sometimes arrange an early showing for a few visitors. An actor sitting beside a bed must get the whole joke into a glance; carrying a favourite scene to someone recovering from illness can produce a better performance than a crowded room does.

Family resemblance makes casting intimate. A former partner may remain the best person to play someone long after they have stopped sharing a home. Children grow up with public versions of their parents and eventually try replacing them. A newcomer is delighted to discover that a neighbour has learned her habitual pause, until the pause becomes the only thing anybody asks to see.

Vessa has played her brother Orrik for years. Her Orrik saves everything against some future need, including the water in which he has washed a borrowed cup. The real Orrik used to supply her with better lines. Since taking in his partner's two children, he has begun spending freely on their clothes and small pleasures. Vessa keeps the old character because it is her most requested part. When Vessa sees a new coat, she performs Orrik fainting at the price. The children have started hiding purchases from her; they dislike seeing his gifts turned into things he must regret. Orrik wants her to see that the joke has become inaccurate. Vessa thinks he has forgotten how much he enjoyed making it with her.

Other performers want house plays that strangers can follow. Visiting [[Adventurers|adventurers]] occasionally pay for a showing, but a roomful of goblins from the next settlement is already a demanding audience: they do not know whose cup that is. Some actors invent recurring households with no single living model. Vessa considers these thin entertainment. Her partner has quietly begun writing one.

## Unresolved hooks

- Orrik offers to play Vessa in her next showing. She must decide whether to rehearse with him before she knows what he intends to do.
- Two neighbouring households book the same actor for the same evening, each believing a recent romance has settled where her loyalties lie.
- Vessa's partner has finished a play whose best character is a generous father. The first available performance space is Orrik's floor.
