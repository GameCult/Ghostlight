# The Waking Houses of Clayfold

In Clayfold, a child waking frightened can usually find someone making breakfast. Neighbouring households keep overlapping sleeping hours, and many children grow up eating their first meal in a different kitchen from the one where they went to bed. Adults call these neighbours their waking kin. They remember who liked the crust soft, who needed silence before speaking, and who claimed to hate being fussed over while sitting firmly within fussing distance.

Clayfold is a proposed Spoker-descended settlement in the habitable twilight of [[Spoilers/Foundations/World|Kalsa]], beside clay beds and a stream, within reach of grain-growing settlements. Potters exchange storage jars and cooking vessels for food and bundles of dried plant fuel. Kilns stand beyond the houses; covered drying shelves fill the intervening yards. Clayfold's residents include clay diggers, carriers, cooks and people who have never successfully made a pot. Descent matters to some families, but neighbours and spouses can become waking kin without proving ancestry.

The present lives of [[Spoilers/Polities/Spokers|Spoker communities]] remain largely undescribed in the owning note. Clayfold proposes one local answer. Its people take pride in making useful things beautiful: a pouring lip that does not dribble, a bowl whose underside rewards the person washing it. Neither their craft nor their household custom requires knowledge of their remote origins.

## Being awake together

In this country the sun does not set to tell everyone to stop. Clayfold measures household routines with sandglasses, meals and the sounds of work, using local intervals rather than a universal Kalsan day; [[Spoilers/Foundations/Language and Reckoning|Language and Reckoning]] leaves such measures locally variable. Firing a kiln sometimes needs attention across several people's sleep periods. Kin already keeping different hours began sharing meals and watching children. The arrangement became desirable beyond firing work: an ill person need not lie alone while the whole house sleeps.

Two or three neighbouring homes commonly arrange substantial overlap between their waking hours. They change the arrangement when work, health or children change. Sleeping rooms have thick curtains and doors opening away from the busiest yard. Children learn to set bowls down softly before anyone trusts them to carry a full one. Adults admire someone who can entertain a wakeful child without waking five tired people.

Waking kin share food, small repairs and company. They do not all earn together. A neighbour who feeds your child regularly expects flour, fuel, washing or care in return, although nobody expects an infant's exhausted parent to settle things after every meal. Older children move between familiar kitchens with their carers' agreement. Strangers are introduced over several meals before being entrusted with a child. Affection grows through repeated, ordinary presence; an excellent potter can still be a hopeless comforter.

## A household of one's own

Courtship often begins by lingering over someone else's first meal. A lover who changes sleeping hours to meet you makes a visible effort. The same gesture can worry a parent: who will sit with an elderly uncle if both young adults now sleep when he wakes?

Some couples join an existing group of houses. Others rent a small room and relish waking together, eating alone and leaving dishes exactly where they put them. Established families sometimes call this a passing appetite for privacy. Younger residents answer that privacy need not be cured. People living alone may join shared meals while keeping their doors closed; some simply prefer friends who knock.

The custom persists because familiar company is pleasant and dependable care is hard to replace. Its strain falls on those who find it difficult to refuse. A generous kitchen can become everyone's answer to a restless child. Better-off households can buy fuel and prepared food; poorer ones make their hospitality through more work. Clayfold's sharpest domestic arguments concern who gets uninterrupted sleep and whose wish for company counts as a need.

## Unresolved hooks

- A widowed cook wants to move in with a lover across the settlement. Three children regard her kitchen as home; their parents have never asked whether she wants to keep running it.
- Two adolescent friends want their first independent room together. Their families are delighted until both discover they expected the young pair to remain available for different waking hours.
- A potter offers a neighbour paid meal work during a large firing order. The neighbour welcomes the money but fears their friendship will become something that ends when the order is filled.
