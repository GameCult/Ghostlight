# The Unwatched Sleep

At a funeral in Tammel Steps, someone takes the dead person's usual place on the household sleeping platform. Friends sit close enough to feel the same wooden boards and tap a slow, familiar bedtime rhythm. When the tapping ends, the person lying down is allowed to sleep. The others keep company until they wake. Families call this the Unwatched Sleep: a prayer that the dead have reached a place where they can rest without listening for danger.

Tammel Steps is a small Ratfolk settlement built into a hillside in [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/index|post-Elysium]] Elysium. Its terraced homes stand above enclosed food-growing rooms; residents also make ceramic fittings and repair pumps for neighboring settlements. The shared platforms fit their small bodies, with separate sleeping recesses opening from them. A visitor can join the evening conversation without being invited into everyone's bed.

The people living here belong to one of the many communities outsiders group together as [[Ratfolk]]. Their inherited stories describe a tectonically dangerous world, where a tremor could interrupt sleep and someone had to notice. They still teach children how to distinguish a familiar footstep from an unfamiliar vibration. They also teach them that waking everyone requires a reason. Several grandparents are considered poor examples of this second lesson.

## A Place Where Nothing Needs You

The local faith imagines the dead arriving in a house whose floor will never break. There is company there, and room to be alone. Nobody must earn their bed. Some believers expect to meet particular loved ones; others hope only that whoever receives the dead will know how to comfort them. The house has no agreed builder. Its most familiar prayer asks, “May there be someone awake because they wish to be.”

The funeral sleeper gives mourners something they can actually offer: one living person resting while affection stays nearby. People choose a spouse, a friend, or someone who has spent too many nights nursing the dying. Sleep is welcome, never demanded. Sometimes the chosen person simply lies listening to the boards and gets up hungry. Supper has usually been waiting for some time.

The same practice accompanies recovery from illness and the arrival of a newborn. Children learn its rhythms by resting a hand beside an adult's on the platform. Households vary the pace and spacing; marrying into another household can mean learning that its supposedly soothing rhythm is intolerably brisk. This ordinary familiarity helps carry the funeral prayer across generations. It belongs to bodies people have already held, fed, and slept beside.

## Whom The Dead Still Owe

Davin used to tap beside his daughter until she fell asleep. Since his death, his partner Heth has told the child that he is still listening. She means it sincerely. She hopes death has left him able to love them, and that love includes noticing when their daughter calls.

Heth's sister Ulmi wants to lead his funeral prayer. She believes asking Davin to watch the household would leave him on duty forever. She offers to sleep beside the child herself. Heth accepts the help and remains angry: another warm body does not answer whether Davin still cares. Ulmi, who remembers him exhausted, wants his promised rest to include freedom from being useful.

Others here pray to their dead quite freely. Some attend the sleep without believing in any further house. Knowledge of [[Spirits]] complicates these conversations: certain minds can persist in the [[Aether]], but a projection consumes energy and can fade. The faithful disagree about whether such persistence has anything to do with the rest they hope for. No visitor has demonstrated a route to their promised house.

## Unresolved Hooks

- Heth has asked for Davin's bedtime rhythm at the funeral, followed by their daughter's request that he stay nearby. Ulmi has yet to say which words she will speak.
- An elderly platform maker wants her funeral held in the private room she saved for. Her friends must decide how to give her the shared farewell she loves without moving her back into the crowded household she chose to leave.
- Davin's daughter has begun teaching his rhythm to a friend. Heth hears them through the boards before she knows whose hands are tapping.
