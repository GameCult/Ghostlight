# The Sling Yard

At the sling yard, a learner's first sale is usually something small enough to carry under one arm. A basket handle or bundle tie earns little, but someone takes it away and uses it. The yard's workers remember their first customers with embarrassing accuracy.

The yard stands in a proposed provincial market under the [[Spoilers/Polities/Ju'onai Hegemony|Ju'onai Hegemony]], where settled [[Spoilers/Polities/Channeling - Sarxe|Sarxe]] families and their neighbours have repaired carriers' equipment for several generations. Some families arrived with carrying work; others supplied cord, rented working space, or married into the trade. Their descendants now make slings together: loops and broad woven supports that hold a load against a person's back or a pack saddle. They admire a sling that lies comfortably, packs flat, and can be repaired far from its maker. Fine patterned work also sells. There is considerable argument over whether a handsome sling ought to look handsome when empty.

Learning begins with sorting cord, opening worn joints, and making small replacements. To splice a loop, a worker separates the rope's strands near its end, turns the end back, and works the loose strands into the standing rope. Fingers must keep track of what passes over and under while the joint is drawn snug. Learners practise on short offcuts before using the long, sound lengths that cost the yard most to replace. Finished carrying gear is inspected and tried under load; a neat surface alone earns no sale.

Kesri, a Sarxe splicer, feels a nearby pull beginning to travel through a joint. Her Channeling lets her anticipate its movement and redirect force while it is active. It cannot tell her the condition of a coil lying untouched in a cupboard. She still picks apart suspect fibres, watches for wear, and tests her work. She loves the moment separate strands become one firm loop. She also makes a poor instructor whenever orders are late: she catches a learner's uneven pull, steadies the work, and says “there” after the useful moment has already passed.

Her partner Orven sometimes has an opening, the sudden bounded understanding studied by the [[Spoilers/Institutions/Ju'onai Schools of the Opening|Schools of the Opening]], while they try a sling on the loading frame. Sometimes he understands which connection makes one side take too much of the weight. He can explain a change in the arrangement. He cannot make his fingers remember the strand sequence by explaining it harder. Once the frame, loading, or relevant interval changes, his earlier insight may no longer answer the question.

Orven came with family money for practice cord. Kesri came with years of paid repair work and wants to design fine slings of her own. They began exchanging skills because she could make his suggested joints and he could help investigate why her wider patterned supports twisted under load. Neither wanted a permanent assistant. They now finish excellent work together, which is becoming inconvenient: customers request the partnership's familiar designs while both partners want time to attempt work they cannot yet do well.

Their worst quarrel concerns a beautiful sample Orven believed he had made. Kesri had quietly helped its final tightening. She remembers saving expensive cord; he remembers being allowed to sell himself a small fiction. When he demands an untouched attempt, she agrees, then insists that he also sort and prepare his own material. His money buys replacement cord; she has been supplying the extra preparation time as part of their exchange, and is beginning to resent how much it takes.

Other yard workers see different futures here. Experienced makers want the profitable joint orders. Younger workers want access to Orven's test frame and enough good cord to learn on, rather than endless scraps that fray before a lesson is finished. The cord seller offers a cheaper bulk purchase of their usual heavy cord. Taking it would stretch wages further, but leave less money for the finer cord Kesri wants and the sound lengths younger workers would consume in practice.

## Unresolved hooks

- A carrier will pay for Kesri's broad patterned sling, but wants Orven present for every fitting. Kesri must decide whether this is a route into design work she wants.
- Orven's next independently made loop holds on the frame and sits badly on its buyer. The buyer offers to return for alterations if he accompanies one ordinary carrying round.
- The bulk cord offer expires while the younger workers are preparing a shared request for full-length practice material. Kesri and Orven have enough influence to choose which expense the yard undertakes.
