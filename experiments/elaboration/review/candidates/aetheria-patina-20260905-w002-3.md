# The Long Mouth

At the Long Mouth, a small shadow theatre in the Pallas Bloom Cluster, people recognize a romantic lead by the ridiculous size of its jaw. Two curved pieces of dark sheet become a mouth large enough to swallow the lover standing beside it. For years, the favourite ending has been for the mouth to close around a gift, reopen, and reveal that it has accidentally eaten the scenery. Recently, audiences have started laughing before the gift appears. The jaw itself has become the joke.

The theatre belongs to a circle of engineered cephalopod yard workers and their neighbours in the 2760s, several decades after the [[Pallas Species Strikes]]. They rent an evening room on an [[Aeronautics Unlimited]] ramp: one of the company's continuing habitat and yard developments. Most earn their living maintaining seals and construction equipment. Some have ties to the [[Cephalopod Syndicate]]; others want a tolerable shift and an audience that remembers their entrance.

## Making A Shadow

A lamp throws the performers' silhouettes onto a translucent screen. The audience sits on the opposite side. Movable stands carry flat jaws, doors, crowns, and other scenery; performers bring pieces into the light or turn them edge-on until they nearly disappear. A creature can lose its enormous crown and acquire a tiny, mortified head in the same movement.

These are descendants of industrial lines supplied by [[BioElevate]]. They perform in the humidity skins and mobile support rigs that sustain them outside water. Props have separate stands and broad grips; scenery hangs from the theatre frame, clear of life-support connections. Each act is arranged around the reach and comfortable movement of its particular performers. A partner can carry the other half of a monster. Nobody needs to present a complete creature alone.

The older style delights in crowded transformations. An arm holds a lover steady while another moves a door, a third brings on an unwanted relative, and a partner supplies the relative's indignant hat. Good performers keep the story readable while several things threaten to happen at once. Regulars return to see whether a familiar disaster will be escaped this time. It usually will not, but the timing matters.

Door takings pay for the room, lamps, screen repairs, and a modest share for the acts. Neighbours lend props, sell replacements, argue about performances, and book favourites for private celebrations. Young spectators start with a single hinged mouth on a stand. Some grow into performers; others become exacting audience members who can identify a borrowed entrance almost immediately.

## An Unfashionable Jaw

A newer style leaves much of the screen empty. Two small silhouettes approach, hesitate, and turn away; a slight change of angle can make an offered object look like a refusal. Its followers admire restraint and the nerve to hold a pause. They call the crowded style “cupboard theatre,” because everything its performers own eventually comes out.

Sera, who operates the Long Mouth's famous jaw, genuinely likes the new work. She wants to perform a quiet courtship piece with her longtime partner, Pell. Pell thinks her pauses are beautiful in rehearsal and unreadable from the back row. He also knows that their jaw act still sells private bookings. He wants it on the public bill, where new customers can discover it.

Sera has begun accepting applause with a comic bow that tells fashionable spectators she knows the act is absurd. Pell dislikes this more than an outright refusal to perform. He is playing the lover sincerely; she is inviting the audience to feel superior to it. They must decide whether to change the act, split the bill, or admit that they now want different kinds of laughter. Their shared prop cupboard remains locked with a latch either can open.

## Unresolved Hooks

- A young performer asks to borrow the jaw for a parody and offers Sera the starring role. Pell has already agreed to lend it, believing the request was for a revival.
- A family books the old courtship ending for an anniversary. Sera discovers that the supposedly accidental destruction of the scenery was the moment one partner first decided to propose.
- The quiet piece receives its first public slot. Someone in the back row laughs during its longest pause, and Sera must decide whether to use the laugh or wait it out.
