# Longbank Clay Walks

At Longbank, an elephant community near the farms of Hellas Basin on Mars, families make landscapes in clay along the sides of a broad indoor walk. An adult can explore a ridge with a trunk while a calf works at a lower shelf beside them. Someone arriving after a long absence may find the bend of a childhood stream waiting under their touch, with a new bridge made by a neighbor they have yet to meet.

Longbank belongs to the [[Elephant Enclave]] in late Sol, during the twenty-ninth century. Its residents include uplifted elephants and human households. They maintain freight equipment and grow fodder in cooperation with nearby [[Aya Collective]] farms. Their clay walks occupy a warm, pressure-sealed communal gallery adjoining the residential halls. The space is wide enough for elephants to pass without interrupting someone working at a shelf; human walkways reach the same work from the other side.

The Enclave's commitment to memory takes a particular form here. Residents preserve places by repeatedly making and changing them together. These are remembered or invented landscapes, not reliable survey models. An aunt can insist that a hill was steeper when she was young. Her nephew can ask whether this happened before or after she became famous for climbing it.

## Learning Someone's Landscape

Children begin by making a place another person can explore. A teacher might ask for a sheltered hollow, three distinguishable paths, or a crossing that can be found without being pointed out. Working with a trunk rewards broad surfaces, changes in texture, and curves that can be followed continuously. Tiny decoration has admirers, but a beautifully detailed town that falls apart when touched is considered an unfinished invitation.

Visitors learn to ask before touching. Some makers welcome alterations; others want company while they work. An older child may offer a younger one a bank to shape alongside theirs, taking responsibility for teaching how wet the clay should be and helping repair the inevitable collapse. Such partnerships often continue after the lesson ends. Families speak of a child's first patient teacher with an affection usually reserved for relatives.

Human makers use hands and long tools. They sometimes favor details their elephant companions find fiddly, while elephant makers can underestimate how exhausting a large surface is for a human child. Adjustable shelves and smaller removable trays let neighbors work together without demanding identical movements. The practice belongs to this community; [[Uplift]] encompasses many bodies and histories, and neither elephant ancestry nor Enclave membership makes someone enjoy clay.

## Making Room

Clay can be soaked and used again. Covers keep it workable between visits, and residents share the cleaning and water recovery. The expensive provision is the habitable gallery itself. Households support it through their common maintenance budget because it gives children, resting workers, and elders somewhere to spend time together without arranging a separate visit. Retired residents often teach there, but younger shift workers also take classes, and nobody earns their place by becoming an instructor.

The difficult question is when to remake a beloved stretch. A landscape may hold decades of additions: a child's first arch, a partner's improbable mountain, a hollow someone used to smooth while talking through bad news. Its original makers can be gone while several families still visit it. Photographs and scans preserve its shape. They do not preserve the weekly act of tending the same clay with someone you love.

Some households keep small fired pieces at home and willingly surrender their gallery space. Others maintain whole stretches for years. Younger makers disagree among themselves: some want room for unfamiliar designs; others are the fiercest defenders of the landscapes their grandparents taught them. Renting another hall would preserve more, but requires more heating, cleaning, and paid work. Longbank has no agreed age at which affection becomes an unreasonable claim on space.

## Unresolved Hooks

- A young maker offered a traveling exhibition wants to take the original clay. Their teaching partner assumed they would keep tending it together at home.
- A family proposes firing an entire much-loved landscape after its oldest maker dies. Its youngest contributor still considers it unfinished.
- A spare freight hall becomes available. Residents must decide whether a second walk is worth the extra maintenance shifts, and who will actually take them.
