# The Keshet Saltfolk

*Canon candidate.*

When carriers return to Keshet, families heat the outdoor ovens before the baggage is unloaded. Salt has gone down-valley; flour has come home. Children look for seed cakes while adults listen to grain pouring into bins. Most saltfolk are [[Humans|human]], descended from pan workers and mule carriers who began staying through the winter beside a shallow inland salt basin.

Their towns stand on gravel slopes near freshwater springs. Below them, salty seepage feeds shallow clay pans where summer sun dries it into crystals. The springs water households and small bean patches, with too little left for broad grain fields. Salt buys flour, fodder, timber and iron. Dung and thorn scrub fuel the ovens; roof beams pass through several generations of thick earthen houses.

Before the morning glare becomes painful, workers mend pan banks, guide brine and scrape salt beneath rain covers. Children learn to recognize grit in crystals and damp in purchased grain. Families with inherited pans can give a married child a livelihood. Newcomers may build along the unused basin edge with neighbours' help, repaid in labour, but must eat through weeks without a harvest. Adra hires out to established owners while saving for her own pans. Some fellow workers prefer wages and a winter free of repairs. She wants something she and her husband can work together.

He repairs carts down-valley. Their savings will buy a donkey; keeping it fed and buying a cart will take more. Adra hopes it could pull a larger saleable load than it could carry, provided the road has gentle enough slopes and a firm, drained surface. The present mule path offers neither throughout. Her husband will not promise that a cheap animal makes cheap freight.

The path climbs from Keshet to a pass, then descends through two villages before reaching the largest valley market. Those villages, mainly [[Halflings|halfling]] farming communities, supply grain and fodder as well as customers for salt. Bek takes six mules through them. His sister married into the first; in the second, his friend Sella keeps a dry granary and a room where Bek recovered from a broken leg. He brings her spare harness fittings and buys her small grain lots alongside larger purchases. She has advanced flour through a failed salt season because she knows whom he will repay first.

Carrier households admire someone who remembers every requested purchase. At home, people boast about a roof that needs no borrowing to mend, or enough pots when cousins arrive. Bek enjoys the journeys themselves: the kitchens where his bad singing is expected, and the news he can carry between relatives who seldom travel. Adra likes those people too. She has travelled with him. She would like more evenings with her husband at their own oven.

The road supporters propose two works. They would widen the shared basin climb and replace its steepest bends with longer turns. Beyond the pass, they would cut a new road along a gently descending shelf, joining the market road below both villages. The shelf has no through track today. This second work creates the bypass; widening the existing route alone would still take travellers through the villages. Making the existing village descent cartable would require further long turns and retaining walls which the supporters have not offered to fund.

Pan owners pledge grain for the crews, expecting larger sales. Adra hopes to carry her own salt. Bek wants fodder stores first: hungry mules already limit winter journeys. He also fears losing the small purchases that make his village rounds pay. Sella could sell to other buyers, but fewer visits would leave her buying fittings separately and Bek with less trade to offer when he next needs flour on credit. Keeping the old path open would not keep those exchanges worthwhile.

Adra's husband brings a narrow borrowed cart to a firm stretch below the climb. The donkey moves the trial salt load there; at a loose bend they unload half. Nobody has yet hauled grain back over the pass or laid the proposed shelf surface. Adra asks what that road would cost to keep firm after rain. Bek stays to help reload.

## Hooks left

- Sella offers roof timber for a cart branch to her village. Its descent needs surveying, and Adra's planned cart would need some of that same timber.
- The next trial must bring grain home uphill. Borrowing enough grain for a meaningful load depends on Bek asking his village hosts.
- A wet drying season prompts comparisons with reports of [[Surface Symptoms|disrupted weather]]. Its cause remains unknown; the pledged road grain may be needed for supper.
