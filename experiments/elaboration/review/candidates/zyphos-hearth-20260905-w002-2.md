# Turn Mantles

*Canon candidate. Split Bough and the practice name are provisional.*

At Split Bough, an interior neighborhood of the [[Airawa Empire]], a beautiful mantle can look unfinished until its wearer climbs. Two bands of dyed cloth meet across a turning back; a folded panel opens beneath a raised upper arm; the small lower hands pull a binding and divide one pattern into three. Someone watching from the next branch catches a design that a spectator below never sees. Being shown the best part can feel very personal.

Split Bough's households fit clothing and climbing equipment for imperial service. Their homes and work platforms occupy neighboring branches of a managed living tree network. During breaks, wearers try new arrangements along the short climbs between platforms. People call these garments turn mantles. Most are ordinary mantles refolded and rebound, with loose edges kept clear of talons and working joints. Their attraction lies in the sequence of appearances, rather than expensive material alone.

An [[Airawa]] has the body for this pleasure: taloned feet and powerful upper arms anchor and brace, while smaller lower hands can manipulate ties. A wearer keeps their weight supported while opening a panel. The admired skill is timing. A premature tug spends the surprise; a late one leaves the intended admirer studying the wrong shoulder. Children watching from a platform try to predict which color will appear next. Adults who consider themselves above guessing are often quite specific about the answer.

Clothing already makes ecological relationships legible in [[Pre-Imperial Airawa Tribes|Airawa contract dress]], and imperial fashion orders those signals into ranks. Split Bough makers keep the required identifying arrangement visible while varying the folds around it. Some love a crisp, balanced display of their standing. Others prefer patterns that become briefly uneven and then resolve. Both can consider their taste thoroughly imperial. They disagree over what accomplished service ought to look like: immediately recognizable, or confidently inventive.

The neighborhood's pleasure has a practical place within [[Imperial Biopunk Infrastructure|imperial production]]. Fitting garments to bodies that must grip, brace, and work demands judgment. Local employers allow the trials as practice in fit and movement, and use successful arrangements in service clothing. The break-time audience adds its own stakes. A maker wants a particular wearer to choose their mantle again. A wearer wants the neighbors to recognize a turn before recognizing who supplied the cloth. Some residents prefer a plain wrap and an unobstructed view of everyone else's exertions.

Inviting someone onto a particular watching branch is a small act of attraction. It says that an appearance has been prepared for their angle. Friends make the same invitations, so an admirer still has to risk asking what was meant. Accepting a view creates no claim to a body or to reproduction; gestation remains subject to the empire's controls over trees, pollinators, and heredity. Local affection proceeds inside those conditions, with all the ordinary opportunities to misunderstand a generous gesture.

One current quarrel concerns a wearer who has begun favoring broad, quiet panels. Their closest companion makes intricate reveals and keeps offering to improve the new mantle. The wearer once loved being the neighborhood's most elaborate moving object. Now they enjoy giving a single color enough space to be seen. The companion hears a judgment on years of shared work. At the next trial, the wearer must decide whether to accept one more clever fold or appear in the simpler arrangement they actually want.

The [[Biological Memetic Engine]] can alter associations and responses here as elsewhere in the empire. Split Bough has no protected reservoir of taste outside its reach. Within the initiative permitted for useful work, however, neighbors still have particular audiences to please, mistakes to make, and appearances they look forward to seeing again.

## Unresolved Hooks

- The quiet mantle receives an invitation to a prominent service display. Its wearer must name a maker; the companion supplied the cloth but opposed every visible choice.
- A meticulous fitter who cannot manage the favored climbing turn recruits a spectacularly unreliable wearer. Their first successful reveal is seen from the wrong branch.
- Two friends accept the same carefully positioned invitation. The host prepared one reveal for each, but only one knows there was meant to be a second.
