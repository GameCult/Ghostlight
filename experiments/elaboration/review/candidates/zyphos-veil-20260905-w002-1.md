# The Unanswered Scraps

*Canon candidate. The locality and custom below are proposed; names are provisional translations.*

At Splitroot, a lantern grove on the [[World/Sa'ueia Continent|Sa'ueia continent]], several lattice-ant colonies spend part of their well-fed hours investigating things nobody has paid them to explain. The glue around a fiber records warmth on only one side. A scrap carries two pressure marks where one crossing was expected. The colony that arranged the little event knows how it happened. Its neighbors want the pleasure of finding out.

These are questions small enough to walk around. [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Lattice Ants|Lattice Ants]] think most strongly when their jointed bodies lock into spans, loops, and sheets, joined with shed fibers and microbial glue. The glue holds brief impressions of pressure, heat, immune residue, and disturbed minerals. Around a puzzling scrap, a colony assembles the body with which it will examine the evidence. Another colony waits its turn nearby. They keep their lattices separate; sharing a question does not require sharing a mind.

Splitroot's colonies earn sugar, shelter, and material for their brood through ordinary diagnostic and recycling work. Their [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|lantern-tree]] hosts need ground histories, and the neighboring [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle roads]] need contamination reports. The unanswered scraps occupy sheltered patches beside the nests, away from working trails. Their makers use clean, permitted materials and staged events. A real client's concealed history is a poor invitation to play.

A good puzzle offers several plausible sequences and one small trace that distinguishes them. Its maker might press a glue-coated shed fiber twice, warming the coating between presses, then offer the resulting patch for inspection. Participants indicate an answer by performing the sequence they think produced it. The maker can repeat the original event while they examine it. This gives disclosure a physical form, without requiring a sentence-by-sentence translation between colonies.

Some colonies admire speed. They unfold around a sample, withdraw, and immediately attempt a reconstruction. Others keep returning to a stubborn scrap between jobs. A successful maker earns invitations to inspect other colonies' puzzles; a colony that always reveals the answer too soon gets fewer. A colony can refuse the offered demonstration by withdrawing its inspecting lattice, then return later to try again.

The most admired discoveries are sometimes failures of the maker's explanation. A visiting colony produces the same traces by a sequence the maker never considered. Both demonstrations work. The scrap was less informative than everyone thought, and suddenly it is interesting again.

There is disagreement over how long that interest deserves protection. One Splitroot colony, provisionally called Bent Span, likes to leave two explanations standing until fresh evidence becomes available. Its neighbor, Quick Loop, asks for the demonstration while the impressions remain readable. Quick Loop considers an answer lost to fading glue a wasted opportunity. Bent Span considers some of Quick Loop's answers guesses dressed for work.

Their quarrel matters beyond the puzzle patch. [[Biosphere/Couriers and Pollinators/Threadwing Couriers|Threadwings]] bring permitted shed fibers and sometimes witness the staged events. One courier has begun repeating that Bent Span can discover crossings which leave no trace. Bent Span has demonstrated only that a particular trace admits two explanations. The rumor attracts paying questions that cannot be answered. Correcting it means allowing outsiders to watch a celebrated puzzle fail to distinguish its own possible endings.

The colonies also differ over spectators. Quick Loop welcomes them: an accurate demonstration can win a client. Bent Span wants the unfinished question kept among those still working on it. A visitor who blurts out the maker's sequence may be truthful and thoroughly unwelcome. But neither colony can make missing evidence return by keeping the explanation secret.

## Open Questions

- The courier offers to repeat the correction along its route, but wants Bent Span to demonstrate the uncertainty in front of a prospective client. Will the colony expose its limits publicly?
- Quick Loop has reproduced Bent Span's favorite puzzle by a third sequence. Will it show the result immediately, or let its rival finish looking?
- A lantern-tree host remembers an incidental movement beside an old puzzle. Its account might settle the question, although the original glue impressions have faded. Will either colony accept testimony it can no longer check against the scrap?
