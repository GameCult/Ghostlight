# Latch Row

Latch Row is a short residential passage in the inhabited ring of Morrow Port, in [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/index|post-Elysium]] Elysium. Its homes have broad front rooms where human and [[Ratfolk]] neighbors can sit together, followed by private rooms fitted to the people who live there. A visitor may know a household for thirty years without seeing where anyone sleeps. Residents disagree about whether that is a comfortable friendship or an unfinished one.

The passage has a level floor, low and high door controls, and enough width for two people carrying furniture to pass. Behind the front rooms, space becomes less generous. Some homes divide into low chambers with enclosed sleeping shelves; others retain human-height bedrooms. Ventilation must reach every partition, and small rooms have their own usable escape route into the passage. Thick doors and padded fittings soften household noise, but cannot conceal every footstep. An invitation to listen to music is not permission to turn up the volume until the next household participates.

Morrow is a small repair and supply port. Row residents mend clothes, provision visiting crews, service domestic equipment, and do specialist work for customers including the [[Daedal Houses]]. Their housing association buys air, water, power, and maintenance from the port. It controls interior fittings, not the ring's life-support plant. Keeping a large front room costs rent and heating even when nobody visits; the smaller chambers behind it help some households afford the arrangement. Other residents would gladly pay for another bedroom instead.

The first households fitted the row together after years of visiting across badly matched doorways. Their children grew up with supper tables that had seats at several heights and with friends whose parents could not follow them through every door. The resulting custom is local. Neither small bodies nor a Ratfolk ancestry determines whether someone likes an enclosed room.

People take considerable pleasure in choosing what a guest encounters. One front room is all pale cloth and adjustable lamps, another crowded with painted cupboards. A household that likes strong cooking smells closes its inner kitchen door before receiving a neighbor who finds them exhausting. Someone who prefers brightness may keep a brilliantly lit private room behind a shaded entrance. Residents admire a host who remembers how a particular friend likes to sit. They also admire rooms that look inhabited. The argument about whether spare charging leads belong in a decorative bowl is now older than several marriages.

Privacy has an affectionate form here: friends help make rooms they will never enter. They carry panels as far as the front room, sew covers to measurements, or lend tools without demanding a tour. Children learn to ask before following someone indoors, including through openings they can easily use. A closed inner door can mean wanting a nap, wanting company from somebody else, or wanting to arrange one's possessions without receiving advice.

This does not satisfy everyone. Adra, a human seamstress, helped raise her Ratfolk niece Kivet in a household whose sleeping rooms were all human-accessible. Kivet now wants a home with low, warm chambers and cupboards reachable from her sleeping shelf. She has saved for the partitions herself. Adra had imagined helping her unpack and staying late among the half-open boxes. The offered front-room supper feels painfully like becoming a visitor.

Kivet could retain one tall inner room, but it would consume the space she wants for a private sewing room. She likes her aunt's company; she also wants somewhere to finish a garment before Adra suggests how it ought to hang. Adra knows this and has stopped offering suggestions aloud. Kivet still hears the pause.

Some families keep every room mutually accessible because wandering into one another's company is their favorite part of home. Others protect a guest bedroom at considerable expense. Latch Row makes either choice familiar, without making it cheap.

## Unresolved Hooks

- Kivet has asked Adra to make the curtain for her sleeping shelf. Adra has bought the cloth but has not asked for its measurements.
- A vacant home could give the row a shared guest bedroom. The household beside it wants the same space for a child's first private room, and has already brought over a bed.
