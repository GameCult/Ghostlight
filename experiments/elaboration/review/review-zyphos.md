# Counterroot Embassies

Some [[Matriarch Trees]] admit a rival's brood of memory-bearing borers into their outer archive roots. A **counterroot embassy** feeds on damaged cambium, infected grafts, and weakly bound memory layers. Its pruning helps keep [[Biosphere/Parasites and Symbionts/Mirror Amoebae|mirror amoebae]] from making false familiarity resemble healthy tissue.

Residues of what it eats survive in gut symbionts and resin-cased waste passed to [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle roads]]. The report is chewed, incomplete, and limited to what the host exposed. It can show that an archive layer was sacrificed without recovering the lives remembered there.

The brood needs its host's sugars and tissue, together with periodic resin from the sponsoring Matriarch, to reproduce. [[Biosphere/Couriers and Pollinators/Threadwing Couriers|Threadwings]] carry the resin. The road and couriers can interrupt the compact as effectively as either tree.

## Mutual Infestation

Rivals usually exchange broods. Each accepts a foreign-aligned colony that maintains its roots and carries evidence of their condition outward. Airawa healers rely on the pruning around gestation roots; families remembered in faint outer cambium fear becoming the next layer declared unhealthy.

The host can starve its embassy or direct it toward worthless tissue. The sponsor can withhold resin. Broods can conceal a rot pocket, overfeed, or seek a third tree willing to support their descendants. A compact that calls the colony property still needs its cooperation.

The most feared abuse is **false rot**. A keeper induces distress around an inconvenient memory, then invites the brood to remove the sick layer. The foreign colony performs a real repair. Its report begins too late to show who made the tissue ill.

Burning out an embassy leaves the host to replace its pruning and explain the loss to roads and couriers. Those partners may depend on the same castings or resin traffic. A stronger Matriarch can destroy the brood and discover that several smaller neighbours have stopped supplying the things her roots need.

The [[Technologies/Biological Memetic Engine|Biological Memetic Engine]] can exploit access to those wounds. Divided dependence also gives a brood ways to carry signs of interference beyond its host before the host is fully rewritten.

## Open Questions

- Can an embassy leave both trees, and which partners will help it find another reproductive arrangement?
- What evidence of false rot survives outside the tissue already eaten?
- When a sponsor dies, who will supply resin to its indispensable broods inside rival trees?

**Nitpick focus:** how reversible should immune conditioning be, and how long should a supplier need to sustain the gifts before gaining leverage?

# Benefactor Capture

A family can enclose a [[Breeding Grounds|breeding ground]] by feeding it too well.

Under [[Mutable Memory Endosymbiosis]], food carries microbial and memory signatures from the organisms that made and handled it. Repeated safe feeding teaches infant tissues, caretaker grafts, and nursery filters which signatures accompany nourishment. If one circuit of suppliers dominates successive growth cycles, substitutes can become difficult to digest or provoke rejection despite containing the needed nutrients.

A route-rich [[Sa'ueia Family Units|family]] can coordinate sugars, minerals, cultures, and medical feed through the same partners. Roads grow toward the dependable waste. Producers and grazers acquire reasons to defend the arrangement. The family gains leverage through a trophic coalition whose members benefit in different ways.

At first, the gifts keep infants alive and spare exhausted caretakers work. Some donors discover their power only when withdrawal becomes dangerous. By then they can ask for nursery priority, favourable route decisions, or reproductive access. Families offering unfamiliar food find its rejection cited as evidence that they lack standing.

Caretakers can try to keep several sources in use, including foods the nursery tolerates poorly. Doing so consumes care and can slow recovery during a shortage. Refusing an abundant gift hurts someone now; dependence may take years to become unmistakable.

Leaving an established arrangement requires enough alternate nourishment and medical support to carry dependent bodies through readjustment. A benefactor can call that effort deliberate starvation. A road or microbial lineage leaving the coalition may make independence possible while losing its own safest source of food.

## Open Questions

- Who can commit infants and infirm adults to the lean interval required to change suppliers?
- If the [[Airawa Empire]] provides a broadly tolerated rescue culture, what must the ground keep receiving to remain healthy afterwards?
- Can a donor end the capture it created without abandoning the bodies that learned to depend on it?

## Z3 — Future-Kin, consolidated

**Destination:** one new `Eusocial Interbeing/Ecology/Future-Kin.md`, linked from Ecology, Breeding Grounds, and Sa'ueia Family Units. Neither original kinship addition would be imported separately.

**Editorial choice:** one mechanism owns both maturation and later repair. Future-kin are the carriers; counterbroods are the living developmental cultures they carry. Rejected the original fixed three-carrier/two-agreeing rule and exact three-eclipse handover, which imposed one protocol without a species-level reason. Retained bodily refusal, narrowing, and changed cultures. Imperial application remains a possibility rather than newly asserted empire-wide history.

**Nitpick focus:** this is the cohort's largest biological commitment. Does carrying another person's possible development make kinship sufficiently alien, or should the practice be rarer and elective? The draft makes it an established Sa'auei'a care practice, with variable arrangements.

---

# Future-Kin

Sa'auei'a breeding grounds entrust some of a child's developing body to other organisms. Caretakers cultivate **counterbroods**: living memory-bearing cultures that keep a possible organ change, immune accommodation, or metabolic adaptation viable outside the child. The organisms carrying them become **future-kin**.

The practice grows from [[Mutable Memory Endosymbiosis]]. A developing body settles on particular relationships among tissues and symbionts. Some unused arrangements can survive elsewhere and later return during maturation, healing, or a change of ecology. A counterbrood holds one such arrangement. It cannot reproduce a whole person.

## Keeping a Future

Breeding-ground specialists distribute cultures among hosts with different tolerances and vulnerabilities. Sa'auei'a relatives may carry them in medical grafts; [[Biosphere/Parasites and Symbionts/Burden Flowers|burden flowers]] can keep particular immune and stress responses; other partners accept cultures suited to their tissues. The number of hosts and degree of overlap depend on the development being kept. A family lacking compatible carriers has fewer choices to preserve.

Every host feeds and changes its counterbrood. A culture kept for years in a different species returns with part of that species' history. Specialists compare overlapping cultures where they have them, but resemblance alone cannot establish that a change will suit the receiving body.

Family routes bend toward growth intervals and the organisms needed at them. A young person can depend more immediately on a grazer visited each season than on a genetic relative. Carriers receive nourishment, care, and lasting attention from the family. They may also refuse a transfer their own tissues cannot survive.

## Handover

A new carrier first grows the culture alongside the old one. The dependent's tissues sample the change while caretakers watch for rejection. Only then can the departing host safely let its culture decay. Handover duration varies with the bodies involved; a travel deadline does not make them compatible sooner.

If a carrier dies or a route closes, the dependent may continue growing with fewer viable developments. A later injury becomes harder to repair. A once-possible symbiont partnership can no longer be tolerated. Families mourn these **narrowings** without a corpse. The person remains, and an adulthood they might have inhabited is gone.

Future-kin also acquire means of coercion. A carrier can demand a return visit, a family can threaten to stop feeding a reluctant host, and cooperating keepers can alter the choices presented to a dependent. Emergency cultures at a [[Breeding Grounds|breeding ground]] can replace some losses, bringing the commons into a kinship it must then sustain.

The returning person can refuse a counterbrood. The carrier may have lived with it long enough to resist its destruction. Neither body can be treated as a storage vessel without someone being injured by the decision.

## Open Questions

- When a grown person wants an unused counterbrood destroyed, can its carrier keep a culture that has become part of its own body?
- If an allied species changes a culture into the only adaptation that can save its dependent, what relationship has it created?
- Could the [[Airawa Empire]] govern someone's later development by capturing their future-kin, and how would foreign conditioning change the culture before it returned?
