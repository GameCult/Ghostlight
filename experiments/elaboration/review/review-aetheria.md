## Keeping The Quarrel

A transit worker's suspended clearance can cost her family its district housing and break her father's access to the clinic that treats him. Her wages continue. She has lost the qualification attached to the apartment. Her partner applies to keep them there, but the housing office assesses them together and returns the same unresolved clearance review.

An independently supported sister can instead receive the father into her own household. She must already have sufficient housing and access to compatible care. She assumes his ordinary support costs, while his existing treatment authorization is transferred to the receiving clinic. His placement then rests on her entitlement and remains valid for as long as she qualifies and supports him. The transit worker's unresolved review cannot, by itself, evict him again or cancel that treatment.

In several Earth arcologies absorbed after the [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Timeline/Events/Urban Sovereignty War|Urban Sovereignty War]], families prepare for this by keeping one relative outside their shared support arrangements. That person pays for a separate home and keeps enough room and income to take in dependants. Families call the practice **keeping the quarrel**.

### The Separate Household

These arcologies assess shared rent, maintenance guarantees, and recurring care as one **support household**, even when its members use several addresses. Pooling lets families share housing allowances and care provision. It also makes their applications inherit restrictions attached to the employment that supports the pool.

Emergency transfers to an outside sponsor exist for deaths, incapacity, and failed households. A sponsor already helping to fund or maintain the failed pool is not independent: accepting another address from the same arrangement would let residents claim the same support twice and disguise a continuation as a rescue.

The housing office can test that financial and caregiving history. Blood relationship, friendship, or a prediction of hidden affection does not make an independently funded home part of the failed pool. The receiving household has its own contract and pays its own costs. Cancelling it requires grounds against that household; the worker's lost clearance supplies none on its own. Administrators retain the distinction because independent relatives keep dependants out of public accommodation and care queues. A separate security or custody order can still prevent a transfer.

### What the Relative Gives Up

Keeping the quarrel means refusing useful arrangements long before an emergency. The spare room cannot become rental income. A relative cannot rely on the family's money to keep it, co-sign its housing, or register as its regular source of childcare while remaining outside the assessed pool. When a parent needs daily help, the future rescuer may have to refuse the role that would make the present bearable.

Occasional visits and celebrations do not defeat independence. A staged argument does not establish it. Auditors can examine shared payments, declared care, and actual residence. The painful separation lies in years of declining obligations that the rest of the family must carry.

When the arrangement is used, the relative takes the dependants and pays for their home. The suspended worker remains outside the receiving household; moving in or making it financially dependent on her would reopen the assessment. A grandmother can keep her treatment and a child can keep a stable home while the parent endures the review elsewhere.

Families with a second reliable income can afford this reserve. Others try to build it after repeated clearance reviews have already emptied their savings. The relative with the most portable work is asked to stay available; a new partner, another dependant, or a job loss can end the arrangement. The family may spend years treating that person's unused room as something already owed to them.

### Open Questions

- When a quarrel-keeper wants a family of their own, who is expected to give up the room everyone has been relying on?
- If a receiving clinic disputes whether it can continue an elder's treatment, who keeps the elder housed while the clinics argue?

# Remainder Charters

In some [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Uplift|uplift]] communities, the company that maintains the nursery also holds a seat in the government. It speaks for children not yet born. Buying the nursery can buy that seat, including the power to stop the community from moving its children's care elsewhere.

## When the Nursery Changes Hands

Parents first encounter the sale as a notice that nothing will change. The specialists who knew their children leave. Two local nurseries become one regional facility. Visits require a longer journey and a missed shift. A developmental programme once included in ordinary care becomes an optional charge.

For an uplift child, the lost service can mean more than an inferior school. Some lineages need cultivated environments, cognitive scaffolds, or specialist care to develop capacities their parents already possess. A nursery can meet its obligation to raise healthy, employable adults while dropping the costly adaptations that let a child live elsewhere or pursue different work. Parents watch the next generation's choices narrow through changes advertised as standardization.

The community finds another provider and votes to move its reproductive archive and nursery equipment. The new owner withholds approval. It guarantees the children's future and says the proposed transfer would endanger it. Its lawyers request a stay while its managers continue cutting the existing service.

Parents can complain about the journey, pay the new charges, and organize a challenge. They cannot simply take their custom elsewhere. The buyer has acquired both the facilities their children need and a vote over whether those facilities may leave its control.

## The Seat Behind the Sale

The arrangement is called a **remainder charter**. Some supplier-built polities accept it as a condition of recognition. Local delegates represent living members; an unelected **Remainder Holder** represents later cohorts and guarantees the support they will need.

The guarantee protects something real. A newly recognized population could otherwise lose its reproductive stock or developmental facilities when a present government sells assets, borrows beyond repayment, or settles a dispute at the next generation's expense. The holder posts a continuance bond that can be called if it fails to provide the support specified in the charter.

Those specifications matter. Preserving a viable lineage and providing minimum developmental care can be much cheaper than maintaining every service families had before the sale. Closing a local nursery may breach no guarantee if the regional facility still meets the charter. An acquirer can reduce costs, add charges above the minimum, and use the difference to repay its purchase debt.

A charter lists the protected assets as its **future estate**: the archive, nursery, scaffold, or other support later members will require. Selling or pledging a listed asset requires the holder's agreement. Families remain free to leave as individuals, but a departure that strands their child's development is a freedom few can use.

The doctrine draws on disputes such as the [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/Portia Line|Portia Line]] hearings, which left protection of a living Portia difficult to separate from ownership of its scaffold. Later corporate courts turned that unresolved question into a reason to give suppliers a place inside uplift governments. The original settlement did not create remainder charters.

## Burden Succession

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/BioElevate|BioElevate]], a habitat operator, or a creator-aligned trust often takes the first Seat because it already controls the technicians and interfaces needed to guarantee care.

The Seat cannot be sold alone. It passes with the duty to provide support, a transfer called **burden succession**. A buyer takes over the clinic, its licences, the guarantee, and the Seat. The approving court asks whether it can keep the promised service running. Parents have no automatic vote on the acquisition. A purchaser that meets the written minimum can qualify even when families expect a much worse childhood from it.

The population can challenge that finding or demand payment from the bond for an actual breach. These remedies are slow where the holder controls the records and specialist testimony needed to establish failure. Families may win a finding after the developmental interval they needed the service for has passed.

## Discharge

A polity can seek **remainder discharge** by demonstrating member-controlled access to the estate and viable alternatives to the supplier's support. The Seat becomes a trustee appointed and removable under the polity's own rules.

Possessing a genome is insufficient when reproduction still requires a licensed clinic. Owning a scaffold helps little if its readers depend on a withdrawn interface. Former nursery staff, neighbouring uplift communities, and [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Movements/Gene Commons|Gene Commons]] clinics build alternatives by training carers, sharing maintenance knowledge, and keeping compatible equipment. Parents must often pay to maintain the existing service while also funding its replacement.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Movements/Species Controllers|Species Controllers]] defend the professional guarantor against majorities willing to gamble with a lineage. Parents bringing photographs, missed appointments, and children whose adaptations were deferred face an institution charged with protecting a generation too distant to testify.

## Open Questions

- Can a polity choose not to reproduce, or would the holder obtain a stay against wasting the future estate?
- When no bidder can preserve every part of an insolvent supplier's support, who chooses which future capacities survive?
- If a [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Biodrone|biodrone]] line begins teaching and producing its own replacements, can it claim a future estate where law refuses to recognize a people?
