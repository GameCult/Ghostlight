## What life is like

The herds get a familiar shelter and nearby feeding; couriers get a dependable stop and recurrent work; a rooted tree receives news from beyond direct root contact. Local admiration concerns continued care for somewhere one cannot directly oversee. Ridge's invitations, herds' preferences and couriers' nesting priorities give the community several viable ways to live without assigning a shared moral personality to a species. Young grazers learn a route by accompanying adults, not by receiving invented doctrinal instruction.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Slant, the road, returning herds and paid couriers can all benefit for different reasons. Continued sugar expenditure competes with expanding Slant's own courier service; delivery time competes with courier nesting and other work. Ridge wants visitors and offers a genuine alternative. No hidden victim or coercive covenant is required. A thinning branch could disadvantage those still preferring the hollow, while indefinite support could keep Slant's ambitions narrow. The road retains its independent willingness to cooperate; exact exchange quantities and viable carrying capacity remain unestablished.
