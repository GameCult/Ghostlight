# Patina — Hollow Shore Listening Cradles

## Idea

Coastal Sa'ueia families fit portable resting cradles to individual bodies so that visitors can enjoy water movements beside a choir reef. Their wish to welcome more guests creates a practical disagreement over shared adjustable equipment, personal comfort, and how much a traveling family can carry.

## What life is like

Travelers repair equipment, exchange coastal knowledge, rest together, and remember how returning guests like to be supported. Children learn to distinguish water movement from faults in a frame. Makers can aspire to fine sensory detail, difficult individual fittings, or broadly useful shared designs. A member can enjoy quiet comfort without sharing a relative's enthusiasm for interpreting every pulse.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Guests gain rest and recognition; makers gain satisfying skilled work; the reef receives the visiting families' proposed mineral contributions within its established exchange pattern. No intrinsic victim or concealed exploitation is proposed. Poorly chosen shared fittings can make some guests uncomfortable or socially marginal, and elaborate personal kits consume carrying capacity and repair time. Equipment occupies a small bare-stone footprint; compatibility with a particular reef's passage and nursery needs must be established locally, not inferred from its perceived friendliness.

## Hooks left

An adjustable frame fails its maker's own teacher; a lost pulse pattern exposes disagreement about what listeners were attached to; an inland departure forces choices about whose comfort the traveling kit preserves. These are invitations for later events, not predetermined disasters or revelations.

## Rejected first ideas

- A wetland color-viewing custom: prismwake flashes already carry encounter accounting, so decorative treatment risked obscuring the mat's existing communication.
- A diving or underwater singing culture: Sa'ueia anatomy is too lightly specified to assume gills, specialized aquatic senses, or human vocal behavior.
- Reef performances summoned for visitors: would require an additional account of the colony's motivation and could turn ecological responses into a convenient entertainment service. Passive listening leaves the reef's independent work intact.
