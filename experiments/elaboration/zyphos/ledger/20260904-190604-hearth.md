# Hearth Elaboration Ledger

## Idea

A Sa'ueia child does not carry their whole adult body inside them: breeding-ground caretakers divide the memory-patterns for still-unformed organs among several Sa'ueia and non-Sa'ueia "future-kin." This makes cross-species care a literal distributed organ, so a caretaker handover must grow the child's missing future into a new body—and route closure, family fracture, or institutional capture can decide what kind of adult the child is physically permitted to become.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Children benefit from developmental memory that can survive one keeper's corruption or death, while non-Sa'ueia keepers gain recognized kinship, standing, and claims to later teaching, repair, or return. Children are harmed by maturation capture, keepers by metabolic burden and exposed immune history, and whole families by routes or quarrels that sever access to a held future; every kin-web costs three willing living carriers, continued nourishment, growth-interval travel, truthful contamination disclosure, and a three-eclipse co-carrying handover.

## Hooks left

- Which non-Sa'ueia species can keep which adult developments, and what ecological payment does each demand?
- Can an adolescent deliberately assemble an adulthood from incompatible held futures without their tissues treating the attempt as coercive memory traffic?
- How will the Airawa Empire classify or exploit a body whose developmental authority remains distributed across persons and ecologies it denies as sovereign?
