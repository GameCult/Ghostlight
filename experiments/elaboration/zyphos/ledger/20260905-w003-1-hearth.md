# Hearth — Company Perches

## Idea

A nonimperial Airawa neighborhood welcomes familiar threadwing couriers into household work spaces for preening and company without requiring an errand. A courier's preference for the resident who does less care makes two close companions reconsider how they share the work and attention of a beloved visitor.

## What life is like

Residents like skilled touch, familiar arrivals, and having another creature choose to remain near their ordinary work. Some enjoy proximity; others enjoy brief visits or keep domestic space separate. A capable caregiver can be proud of their work and hurt by its distribution without demanding control over the visitor. Darem's pleasure is sincere, and Darem still needs to do more of the cleaning. Warm domestic observation is the intended tonal mode.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Couriers receive established useful care and stopping places. Airawa gain chosen company and a craft of attentive handling. Time, cleaning, usable household space, and teaching effort sustain the custom. Unequal care can burden one resident while another receives the welcome; invitations can also crowd a cohabitant's preferred quiet. No inevitable injury or concealed exploitation is needed. The immediate choice is how Ithu and Darem share care and space while accepting that they cannot distribute the visitor's preference between them.

## Rejected first ideas

- Burden-flower companionship: an available ecological partner, but its established disclosures pull this bounded pass toward privacy and grooming territory already well represented in the local history.
- A shared taste or snack custom: threadwing interest in berries is supported, but shared Airawa food physiology is not established; companionship uses a firmer bodily seam.
- A communal versus private preening-room redesign: plausible, but a spatial-policy argument would displace the more particular problem of loving a visitor who prefers one's companion.
- A jealous caregiver restricting access: unnecessary escalation that would make the personal disagreement easier to judge and less interesting to inhabit.
