# Hearth Pass Ledger

## Idea

Sa'auei'a children are not born with all their viable futures inside their own bodies: breeding grounds preserve unrealized immune, metabolic, and sensorimotor states as living counterbroods distributed among several caretakers and allied species. Those carriers become kin with bodily standing over later repair and adaptation, so a caretaker handover is the migration of a dependent's possible selves; a family can lose a child's future without losing the child.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

- **Benefits:** Dependents retain otherwise lost routes to healing and adaptation; breeding grounds prevent any single caretaker from owning a child's whole medical future; willing caretakers and allied organisms gain material support, route access, social standing, and durable kin claims.
- **Harmed:** Dependents can be narrowed by a carrier's death, refusal, capture, or neglect; exhausted carriers may be pressured to keep hosting intimate cultures; Matriarchs and imperial caste offices can convert preserved alternatives into lifelong leverage over families whose active bodies remain outwardly free.
- **Costs:** Every counterbrood consumes nutrients, immune tolerance, privacy, specialist labor, and slow overlap during handover. Because a culture learns from its host, long preservation also risks distortion, disputed ownership, and a future that returns changed by the body that kept it alive.

## Hooks left

- When a mature person demands that an unused counterbrood be destroyed, what happens if its carrier now experiences the culture as part of its own body?
- If a non-Sa'auei'a carrier changes a counterbrood into the only adaptation that can save its dependent, is it a parent, healer, coauthor, or creditor with veto power?
- Can the empire condition a stolen Sa'auei'a counterbrood into a remote instrument of caste control, or will the incompatible culture become a new claimant against captor and family alike?

## Rejected first ideas

- Reassigning a child's care after a mobile family fractures was rejected because the previous Hearth pass explicitly left that succession seam; extending its procedure would be detail, not a new pressure.
- Cross-species adoption witnessed by a burden flower was rejected because shared child-flower custody and trusted companion lineages already occupy that ground.
- A Matriarch storing a deceased relative as a near-complete unborn reincarnation was rejected because it crowds the ancestral-vigil work, collapses cellular states into a copied person, and makes memory hostage in the most expected way.
- Families hiding private memories from the Biological Memetic Engine inside symbionts was rejected as a safe neighbor of revision scars and absence proofs.
