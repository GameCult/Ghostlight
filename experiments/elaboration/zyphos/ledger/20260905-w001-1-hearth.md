## Idea

A threadwing roost community maintains short companion flights for adjusting to regrowing sensory vanes and enjoying one another's company. Its members must choose how to sustain those attachments while pursuing routes that may carry them far apart.

## Underdeveloped territory and evidence

## What life is like

Home includes someone willing to circle back after a missed landing. Young flyers learn by alternating lead and follow; adults play with familiar sequences, practice alone, or maintain friendships beyond shared nesting. Skilled residents and adventurous travelers have different respectable ambitions. Desire to travel can strain a valued attachment without exposing a hidden fraud or villain.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Learners, returning couriers, and companions gain practice, pleasure, and sustained familiarity. Lantern trees gain dependable local pollination and news. Sheltered space and nectar are finite, so leisure competes with feeding opportunities and through traffic. A partner who repeatedly delays departure bears an opportunity cost; one who leaves may lose closeness. Solitary practice remains a viable choice. No group is designated a compulsory victim, and the candidate creates no new office or enforcement apparatus.
