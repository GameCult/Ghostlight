Coastal Sa'ueia families make unfinished pressure duets with a choir reef, carrying playable but incomplete versions inland. A beloved piece mistaken for a promise of hospitality forces its makers to choose how to correct the rumor while preserving a family's private life.

## What life is like

Participants combine repair, mineral gifts, social reunion, patient listening, and portable craft. Children play with extra beats; experts prize attentive responses; accessible forceful versions compete with delicate expensive ones. Families can tour, return regularly, play casually, explain their interpretations, or preserve private origins. The reef retains its ecological priorities and can leave performers waiting.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Travelers gain art they can carry and relationships worth returning for; equipment makers gain a useful craft; the reef receives the proposed circle's mineral offerings without guaranteed participation. Quiet passages favor people with well-fitted equipment and time to learn. False hospitality claims can mislead travelers and burden hosts. Correction risks personal exposure, lost artistic prestige, or abandoning a name audiences love. No disclosure is compulsory, and no secret is required to keep a body or memory alive.

## Hooks left

Misled visitors confront their teacher at the actual reef; affordable equipment removes an interpretively important pulse; a beloved unanswered piece divides those preserving it from those seeking renewed exchange.
