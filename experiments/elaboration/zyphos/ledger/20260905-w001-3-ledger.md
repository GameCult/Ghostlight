## What life is like

Families enjoy returns to their gardens, meals with visitors, construction experiments, and watching apprentices become capable growers. Children learn the difference between edible growth and the generation that must remain. Some adults want dependable coastal provisioning; others want extended inland journeys. Reefs are partners with their own feeding and nursery requirements, not collective machinery owned by the families.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Growers obtain food near shelter; reefs obtain channel maintenance and imported minerals; visitors obtain fresh meals. Mature families can spread harvest risk across inlets and carry provisions through a failure. New growers lack that buffer and may sell harvest labor before they can maintain their own patches. Expanding racks can deprive reefs and downstream nurseries of food or flow. Materials, tending, wet transport, unharvested reproductive stock, and time between returns remain real costs. No closed yield or population budget is claimed: crop productivity and access to other foods must bound later expansion.
