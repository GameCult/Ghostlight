# Ledger Pass 20260904-185101

## Idea

Sa'ueia breeding grounds cannot keep newborn symbiont lineages viable on light, minerals, and care alone; at intervals they must receive the decomposed bodies of adults who died after traveling through ecologies the commons has not recently sampled. This makes a family's last-return right a scarce reproductive asset: itinerant elders can mortgage where their bodies will feed future immunity, while stationary caretakers perform essential living work yet die biologically "common," creating a class pressure no generosity ledger can politely explain away.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

- **Benefits:** Breeding grounds acquire fresh, proven symbiont diversity; newborns remain compatible with distant routes; widely traveled adults can obtain present support for their family units; candle roads receive decomposition work and material.
- **Harmed:** Permanent caretakers are indispensable while alive but carry less bargaining power in death; poor mobile families are pressured into dangerous distance obligations; families with contested or contaminated remains can lose both promised support and funerary autonomy.
- **Costs:** A usable last return consumes a dead traveler's deep symbiont-bearing tissues, fungal quarantine capacity, caretaker labor, and some memorial certainty. Grounds must risk identity echoes and must repeatedly replace lineages that drift toward local nursery conditions.

## Hooks left

- When a choir reef and an inland breeding ground both hold incompatible return claims on one traveler, can the body be divided without destroying the value promised to either ecology?
- Can imperial contact make a traveler's symbiont history simultaneously priceless and inadmissible, allowing the empire to attack Sa'ueia mobility by contaminating returning bodies?
- What collective leverage emerges when permanent caretakers refuse to process rare last returns until their own materially ordinary deaths stop being treated as economically negligible?

## Rejected first ideas

- Another breeding-ground heat, light, or eclipse-reserve ration was rejected because two prior Ledger passes already occupy that ground; changing the rationed input would be safe adjacency.
- Portable trophic credit for newly divided or archive-damaged households was rejected because the previous Ledger pass explicitly reserved that seam.
- A corpse auction between rival candle roads was rejected as the obvious extension of existing decomposer accounting and the earlier road-capacity lead.
- Imperial monopoly on nursery inoculants was rejected as generic siege logic; it did not expose a dependency the native Sa'ueia system creates for itself.
