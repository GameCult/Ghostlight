## What life is like

Members anticipate particular rivals, admire timing, seek contact after a compelling display, and can choose showing, receiving, watching, or abstaining. Calves have something desirable to imitate. Rivalry does not confer authority over migration. Local disagreement is enacted through approach, body orientation, acceptance, and departure; the article's account of motives is author-facing interpretation rather than transcribed speech. Tone: fascination and bodily comedy tightening into a near-term crowd hazard.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Performers gain chosen proximity and attention; receivers gain recognition for a different skill; spectators enjoy a startle within familiar social company. Ordinary contact and available grazing support small gatherings without a new institution or dedicated energy subsidy. Enlarged displays cost spectators composure, can trigger trampling, and expose hosts through their flowers. Mats suffer actual tissue damage and may restrict food; calves and unfamiliar herds face the consequences of signals they cannot safely treat as play. Moving outward preserves spectacle at the cost of greater exposure and distance from feeding water. Staying small gives up some excitement and prestige. The good thing is worth retaining; no concealed atrocity explains it.
