# Ledger Pass — Benefactor Capture

## Idea

On Zyphos, a family can privatize a reproductive commons by feeding it too well. Nutrient traffic carries donor-specific microbial and memory signatures, so repeated gifts can tune a breeding ground's infants, caretakers, and allied organisms to one supplier until rival food reads as contamination and the “benefactor” can threaten famine without owning a grain.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Route-rich Sa'ueia families and their allied roads, producers, herds, and microbial lineages can convert years of real surplus into exclusive traffic and de facto authority over a commons that nobody is allowed to own. Infants and caretakers benefit during the long generous phase, then bear withdrawal sickness; small, newly divided, route-poor, and displaced families find their contributions rejected as biologically suspect. Capture costs the benefactor years of overprovision, coordination across a trophic bloc, and exposure to any indispensable partner's defection; resisting it costs the ground slower growth, deliberate source rotation, metabolically awkward meals, immune stress, and the political insult of refusing useful gifts.

## Hooks left

- When a sentient road, mat, herd, or microbial lineage inside a benefactor bloc wants to leave, is it breaking a contract or liberating a commons it helped capture?
- Who may authorize the dangerous lean interval required to retune dependent infants and caretaker tissues, and which bodies must bear the immune cost?
- Can the Airawa Empire offer a supply culture broad enough to rescue a captured ground while quietly making imperial nourishment the next monopoly?

## Rejected first ideas

- Candle roads auction corpse-processing and disease-filtering capacity between breeding grounds — an earlier Ledger pass names this exact lead, and scarcity auctions are the obvious next move.
- Breeding grounds feed a negotiated predator tithe to preserve population health — strange on first contact, but it requires an undeveloped predator ecology and drifts from exchange into sacrificial law.
- Wealthy families buy anonymity from organisms that eat route traces — too close to revision scars, memory-hostage markets, and the already-landed memory-eating counterroot brood.
- Permanent caretakers strike over the superior value assigned to traveled corpses — a consequence already left explicitly by the Last-Return Rights pass, not a new concept.
