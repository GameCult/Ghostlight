## What life is like

People have favorite growing roads, remember small increments of progress, teach routes through family stories, and take pride in useful relationships that outlast absences. Reliable-route supporters have an equally livable practice. Fungal networks gain nourishment and possible new traffic; their eventual direction can differ from their supporters' hopes. Flowers reproduce or respond to pressure in ways that are consequential without making them miniature Sa'ueia.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Families may gain new connections and the pleasure of helping a place develop; roads gain food and room to grow; flowers may establish offspring. Supporting a reach costs material and repeated travel attention that could nourish dependable roads. Premature publicity risks drawing traffic into an unrecommended passage, while physical investigation can disturb the fungal feeding body. The rumor threatens supporters' credibility even if they sincerely believe it. Broad Amber's continuing success is a real ecological alternative, not evidence of sabotage or villainy.
