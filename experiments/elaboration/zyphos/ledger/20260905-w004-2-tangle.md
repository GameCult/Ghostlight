## Idea

Sa'ueia families and threadwing couriers choose visits together and seek recognition as joint guests. A courier's wish to await a particular acquaintance tests whether its family sponsor will share decisions about the visit as well as responsibility for bringing the travelers.

## What life is like

Members enjoy anticipating unfamiliar places and meeting someone important to a companion. Different bodies make shared travel selective; ongoing craft and courier work support it. Bent Ribbon has a particular acquaintance it wants to visit, while its mate prefers a reliable onward arrangement. Family members can want both their own profitable circuit and a friend's afternoon. The tone is warm curiosity with a concrete political disagreement.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Couriers gain an opportunity for their desired encounters to shape a visit. Less prominent families can acquire direct acquaintances; sponsors gain standing from successful introductions; the receiving tree retains control over its paths. The sponsor risks lost exchange goods and confidence if it must stay to address root damage and fulfill promised nourishment. Sharing remedial work does not automatically transfer the tree's trust. Waiting also consumes time and can separate bonded companions' travel schedules. This is an explicit sponsorship dispute, not a concealed obligation imposed by accepting hospitality.

## Hooks left

A desired courier encounter begins without the ground companions; the tree may still rely on the established sponsor despite another family's offer; bonded couriers may choose different departures after a shared arrival.

## Rejected first ideas

Fluent translated dialogue between the family and cliff couriers would conceal the communication gap. A mandatory guest token or joint legal identity would turn recognition into paperwork. A nesting accident with invented egg chemistry would add biological liability when source-supported root protection already gives the sponsor a concrete stake.
