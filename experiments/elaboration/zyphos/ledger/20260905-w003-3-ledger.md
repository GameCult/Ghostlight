# Ledger — Lowreach

## Idea

Lowreach is a proposed Airawa wetland community whose homes cluster on dry rises to leave feeding light and passage for mats and grazers. Its inhabitants enjoy living within reach of one another, while unequal access to enclosed rooms makes the wish for privacy a material choice between people who still want to remain close.

## What life is like

Residents spend time in adjoining spaces that accommodate simultaneous anchoring, bracing and close hand work. Good hosting involves remembering another body's comfort. Children earn short independent visits; adults tell continuing journeys and invent distant lives. Staying to host and leaving to travel are both worthwhile ambitions. Some prefer constant company, others scheduled visits, and Issik seeks solitude without rejecting a loved sibling. Their grief is personal, with no new claim about death or memory retrieval.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Open feeding space preserves the mat–grazer–fungal relationship and supports reliable routes. Residents gain trade, company and bodily suited homes. Larger established households can already combine privacy and sociability; crowded households must pay in relocation, obligations or a loved shared room to gain the same choice. Private rooms on the remaining dry rise would displace a useful gathering and rest space. Imports, maintenance and dependence on trading traffic make this a supported settlement rather than a self-sufficient platform village. Flood survival and exact engineering dimensions require later placement work; no extraordinary construction capability is implied.
