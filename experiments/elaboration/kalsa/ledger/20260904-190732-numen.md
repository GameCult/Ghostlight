# Numen — Hunger Saints

## Idea

Some Kalsan cults deliberately feed one publicly unbound dead presence at the edge of every major miracle, maintaining a Hunger Saint that is offered the first accessible leakage before unknown feeders can reach the route. Because repeated feeding can build an acquired potential body and open an ascent route, the rite that makes divine power safer is also a god-incubation program: the sponsoring hierarchy gradually creates an independent claimant it cannot safely bind, starve, or destroy.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Cult hierarchies, great-rite beneficiaries, and nearby districts gain a familiar leakage site, an early warning sign, and fewer losses that must be called wholly unknown; named households may gain food, witness wages, burial support, and standing. The cultivated dead, erased or weakly documented claimants, downstream neighborhoods, ordinary petitioners whose grants fund the first share, and workers guarding the side-mouth bear captivity, misrecognition, manifestation hazard, delayed aid, and the possibility of violent stripping. Every opening spends potential before the beneficiary receives it and requires a prepared route, recognizers, mortuary custody, material stops, guards, records, recovery, and an institution willing to keep summoning a dead person whose release would make its safety system fail.

## Rejected first ideas

- A rite for a projection that answers before invocation — refused because the earlier Numen pass explicitly left that safe neighboring procedure.
- A physical closure mark that moves petitions away from a hungry shrine — refused because Public canon already names the relocation and the Patina ledger handed that detail to a later runtime pass.
- A God writing an advance order against its own future will — refused as too close to the Cinder Concord's god-dangerous custody and disclosure machinery.
- Licensed heretics maintained as a God's blind spot — refused because it depended on quietly settling whether a hierarchy can preserve genuinely independent branches among its children.
