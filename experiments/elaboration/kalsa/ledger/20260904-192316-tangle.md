# Tangle — The Borrowed Roster

## Idea

Some Kalsan conspiracies publish their own membership lists before most of the listed people have joined. A Borrowed Roster mixes sworn conspirators with indispensable neutral officeholders and their likely successors, betting that rival precautions will strip the borrowed names of patronage, work, welfare, and trust until the conspiracy's prepared protection is the only survivable allegiance.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Roster authors and their divine, cult, branch, imperial, house, or civic patrons gain access to people and offices they could not safely recruit in private; actual hands gain deniability and intelligence from every precautionary response. Borrowed names, after-names, their dependants, and the constituencies relying on their clinics, roads, archives, grain, rites, or technical work lose livelihood and continuity without any finding of guilt; rival institutions also bleed staff, trust, and supported-division capacity. Each usable false name costs the conspiracy a real secret, a credible harbor, food, lodging, counsel, copied standing, escorts, replacement work, dependant support, and enough patience to profit whether the target is retained, isolated, or removed.

## Hooks left

- What happens when two divine factions borrow the same officeholder and offer incompatible harbors, each relying on the other faction to make refusal impossible?
- Can an after-name refuse succession when the compromised seal is the only thing keeping a clinic, road, ward, or archive alive?
- Which constituency can finance repeated supported division when the accusing faction controls the nearest granary, shrine, safe road, or qualified replacement?

## Rejected first ideas

- A conspiracy whose members are offices rather than officeholders — refused because succession alone was a neat administrative trick with no engine that made exposure more dangerous than secrecy.
- Rival gods secretly sharing a neutral mortal cutout — refused because the Divided City's condominium already makes shared dependence and separated authority do that work at polity scale.
- Fully public records of a divine conspiracy — refused as the safe inverse of the Cinder Concord's distributed god-dangerous archive, already landed by Veil.
- The Cabal as Kalsa's universal hidden government — refused because it is explicitly a noncanonical story seed and would replace local politics with generic furniture.
