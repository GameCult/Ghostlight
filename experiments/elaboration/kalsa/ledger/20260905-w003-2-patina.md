# Patina — The Bowl Asks After You

## Idea

At a small papsenai farming locality, people invite friends back by inventing affectionate complaints from familiar household objects. A beloved exchange lets two friends enjoy their shared history while misunderstanding whether a visit is wanted or expected.

## What life is like

Neighbours who repeatedly eat and sort crops together can recognize particular acts of care in a cup's invented preferences. They enjoy answering familiar objects, explaining old incidents to children, and surprising friends with a new complaint. Some like extravagant exchanges; others prefer one remembered gesture. Repetition, shared possessions and children's participation make the practice cheap to sustain. The present choice concerns how directly to ask for company without losing an enjoyable way of asking.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Friends gain invitations tied to specific memories; hesitant hosts can express affection playfully. New neighbours can misread the invitation as a practical request, spending work on a repair. Frequent teasing can make another friendship or family table feel like disloyalty. Udrem spends extra food and attention while Chavi believes they are still enjoying the same joke. No formal obligation or punishment backs the complaint. A direct explanation can settle a particular misunderstanding, but deciding how much indirection they want in their friendship remains a real difference of preference.
