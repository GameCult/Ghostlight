# Ledger — The Fold Gardeners

## Idea

A cluster of villages grows roots on permanently lit slopes and carries them into ridge-shadow sheds to raise tender edible shoots from their stored reserves. Their shared food culture makes both sides worth living in, while growing prosperity forces choices between export work, independent plots and comfortable homes.

## What life is like

People eat a food they would miss elsewhere, joke through carrying songs, admire patient teachers and learn useful work in another village without losing their family ties. Some farm, some tend sheds, others make vessels, carry goods or cook. The culture persists through a complementary use of nearby growing environments, repeated exchange, learned food preferences and relationships across the ridge. It is not compulsory migration or a species-wide temperament.

The newcomer's central explanation is simple: roots grow in light, shoots finish in shade, and people on both sides need one another. Their argument concerns what a better life would actually contain.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Field owners can choose among buyers; owners on both slopes can absorb losses and extend credit. Renters face sunk preparation costs and weak access to seed-growing land. Carriers spend their bodies making the exchange possible and have reasons of their own to favour investment. Fresh and preserved produce pay differently; jars, transport, water, soil fertility and rot constrain expansion.

The system offers real food, work, sociability and opportunities to learn. It also permits property inequality to grow. Export income could improve housing and paths or concentrate ownership. No hidden atrocity is needed to make the choice consequential.
