## What life is like

People fish, cure catch, repair boats, trade for materials and follow one another's journeys. They admire clear descriptions and useful soundings as well as daring. Warmhook is interesting to its neighbours because crews bring timber orders, news and stories; those neighbours have their own familiar waters and commercial ambitions. A person can belong without becoming an explorer. The warmth between Vessa and Reed Steps survives a genuine political disagreement.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Crews and sellers could gain a larger vessel and court customers. The ruler receives prestige and potentially useful sailors. Hosts gain carriage, knowledge and friendship, but contribute labour, water, heat and landing space. Their willingness to help is neither an entitlement nor a uniform hospitality law.

Preservation imports and freight consume earnings before exploration receives money. Bad weather and unsuccessful fishing remain losses. Patronage could divide fish sellers over commercial access and neighbours over visible alignment. Reed Steps cannot command Vessa's vessel; Vessa cannot promise Reed Steps' launch assistance. A larger boat buys capacity but does not buy cooperation. There is no established monopoly grain supplier or inevitable punishment for refusing the offer.

## Hooks left

A neighbouring guide and accompanying boat seek shared credit at the patron's court. A salt delay puts preservation of current catch in competition with voyage provisions. Both concern choices within the existing economy and relationships; neither resolves the patron's response or the community's allegiance.
