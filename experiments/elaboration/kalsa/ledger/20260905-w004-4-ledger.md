## Idea

A provincial sling yard teaches the same material craft to people who experience its loaded joints differently through Channeling and Analysis. Two partners become profitable together before either has learned what they hoped to learn from the other, making practice time and good cord a contested investment.

## Underdeveloped territory and evidence

The Sarxe owner establishes craft, mixed and settled communities, and close sensing of active force, but gives little account of learning an ordinary trade alongside another Gift. The Ju'onai owner sharply distinguishes causal insight from operational competence; its examples chiefly concern administration and public works. The Schools owner permits learned participation without revelation. Together these leave room for an intimate craft collaboration with genuine, unequal expertise and no transferable magical faculty.

## New claims versus established facts

**Established:** Channeling senses and can redirect active local flows, not remote or dormant sources. Openings disclose consequential connections within a bounded system and interval; changed premises can defeat them. Technical knowledge is not automatically granted. Sarxe settlement under Ju'onai rule and mixed communities are supported. Expensive preparation and patronage confer Ju'onai advantages. Null is absence of expression, not another Gift; the candidate does not assign it a new perception.

**Proposed:** the yard, its shared multigenerational trade history, sling-making materials and equipment, tastes, sales and teaching practices, named workers, personal capacities, family financing, supplier offer and all particular events. The wider culture does not inherit either person's temperament. The specific use of active flow sensing at a tightening joint and an opening during a loaded-frame trial is a proposed craft application of existing powers, not a claim already present in the owners.

**Metaphysical implications:** no new faculty, shared mind, automatic diagnostic power, permanent enchantment, spell-transfer method or god relationship. Kesri's assistance acts during tightening; the resulting physical joint must stand without continuing help. Orven's opening is occasional, bounded understanding rather than a summonable guarantee or a test of future durability. Both still need manual skill and material inspection. The workers are described with readable author labels where needed, but do not speak the backstage axes, signatures or engineered origin. Assistance changes the material event a partner experiences; nobody can treat the resulting success as an untouched demonstration of that partner's skill.

## What life is like

Workers value a first useful sale, comfortable carrying, repairable equipment and fine patterns. Kesri seeks design work; Orven seeks the satisfaction of making what he understands. Their collaboration is wanted and productive, while Kesri's deadline habits and Orven's subsidized material use make reciprocal teaching imperfect. Younger workers have ambitions independent of the pair. Tone: absorbed workplace pleasure, impatient competence and modest class friction.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Carriers get usable gear; the yard earns repeat orders; both partners gain access to capabilities they lack. Existing orders also pay other workers, so abandoning them for experiments has a real cost. Family-funded retries give Orven room for mistakes while consuming Kesri's less visible preparation labour. Scrap-only practice restricts poorer learners. Bulk buying lowers material costs but commits money and work to an existing range. The issue remains production versus learning and design, without inventing an office, grievance system or hidden predatory contract.

## Hooks left

Kesri's first independent design customer still wants her partner present; Orven must fit his work to an actual carrying body; younger workers' request for usable practice stock competes with the bulk purchase. None resolves into a universal correct teaching method.
