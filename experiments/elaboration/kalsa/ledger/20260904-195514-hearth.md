# Hearth — Road Widowhood

## Idea

On several Kalsan roads, a household may declare itself widowed from a living member's service while remaining that person's family. The declaration stops an outside office from treating the hearth's food, replacement labor, child care, debt, sickbed, and patience as free support; if it keeps the worker, it must become guarantor for the absence or admit that its "loyal volunteer" was household levy in ceremonial clothes.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Workers and households gain a way to separate love and kinship from automatic sponsorship of an outside office; dependants gain an inspectable claimant instead of an institution assuming the hearth will absorb every absence. Delving sponsors, military claimants, cults, Prophetic Houses, Techpriesthoods, and shield bodies lose access to hidden household subsidy; poor homes, orphans, refugees, debtors, and estranged recruits are harmed again when institutions select them precisely because no independent hearth can issue the refusal. The practice costs wages, provisions, replacement labor, messages, rescue, recovery, disability support, dependant maintenance, burial, records, witnesses, and the political price of admitting which service cannot survive at its true cost.

## Rejected first ideas

- Expand the branch widow's loyal-welfare bargain — rejected because the prior Hearth ledger named that exact safe seam; it needed an inversion, not more provisions.
- Create a survivor fund for delvers, shield workers, or apprentices — rejected as routine detail already implied by guarantor, support, and dependant-care clauses.
- Establish presumed death for people missing on the road — rejected because Ji'esti procedure already keeps missing, dead, deserted, and captured distinct, while mortuary law deliberately refuses easy metaphysical closure.
- Treat a dangerous cohort as an adoptive family — rejected as the obvious neighbor of existing mobile and work-joined households; it adds warmth without changing who pays.
