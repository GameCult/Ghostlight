# Numen — The Guest Harvest

## Idea

A small papsenai farming community feeds elders from a crop raised without their assistance, letting them experience being cared for before death. Participants disagree about whether this prepares the dead to leave their descendants or to remain as more generous ancestors.

## Underdeveloped territory and evidence

It leaves specific household traditions and tribal rules open. `Death and Binding Law.md`, “What a funeral tries to accomplish,” explicitly supplies differing ancestral and release aspirations, but concentrates on death, custody, and disputed claims. This candidate develops what those beliefs could mean during an elder's ordinary, still-active life.

Local ledger subjects and idea openings were skimmed across the existing 20260904 entries; campaign histories supplied in the assignment were also checked. Prior Numen proposals concern answer rolls, divine shares, Hunger Saints, devotional welcome flowers, and exchanged prayers. This proposal borrows none of their institutions or magical mechanisms. Its crop is a practical transfer of competence and a rehearsal for mortality, rather than a magically maintained welcome garden. Historical novelty is limited to the supplied history and local ledger overview, not an exhaustive archive claim.

## What life is like

The pleasure is receiving food one did not have to organize, watching younger people become competent, visiting without a repair obligation, and discovering that someone else's method tastes good. Children learn whom to ask; younger growers learn to seek help beyond their elder. People can value ongoing ancestral care without wanting ancestral command. Davru wants rest from future demands, while Isket wants a continuing relationship that includes mutual help.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Elders gain a welcome interval of chosen work and dependence. Younger people gain room to decide and visible recognition. The patch costs ground, seed, labor, and potentially another planting if the elder's rescue is needed; keeping it meal-sized limits subsistence exposure. Poor households may struggle to spare even that space, which the borrowed-ground hook retains as a live limitation rather than solving through a new institution. The emotional cost is specific: a feast lovingly earned may fail to give Davru the assurance she sought, while Isket risks promising against her faith. Participation creates no proposed right to strip an elder of work or property.

## Hooks left

The grandfather's cup tests whether remembrance already constitutes invitation in this family. Borrowed ground tests whether a land-poor household can permit a younger grower's choices. The brother's request makes Isket decide whether the same beloved dish should support a religious hope her mother rejects for herself.

## Rejected first ideas

- A funeral rehearsal with rehearsed messages from the dead: too close to exchanged-prayer performance and liable to imply reliable contact.
- Eating the final seed of an elder's crop variety: made horticultural extinction carry the religious disagreement and repeated earlier cultivation-preservation territory.
- A formal transfer of inherited duties: risked becoming another adjudication mechanism. The chosen meal changes a relationship without supplying an office or enforceable covenant.
