# Charter — The Burial of Offices

## Idea

The Divided City's mixed courts can sentence a public office itself to death, because removing a holder is useless when the charter, seal, archive, god-cult obligations, and imperial patronage reproduce the same abuse in the successor. An office funeral turns every later order in its name into an **unquiet act** that any affected commoner can stay and force into provenance review—so rulers who want the dead office's roads, taxes, or grain powers must inherit its unpaid petitions and redress instead of quietly giving it a new name.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Commoners, workers, tenants, hosted providers, and dependants gain a way to attack the mechanism that keeps injuring them across successions without first exposing the hidden patron or shared intent. Rival civic organs also gain a way to destroy a captured lever without handing its succession to Ju'onai, Terjamna, a Prophetic House, or a god-cult; office families, patron networks, holdover staff, and anyone relying on the old seal lose inherited standing and discretionary access. The procedure costs clerks, cross-sector copies, archive guards, interim pay, hearings, service continuity, credential review, and the loss of competent staff; a hostile bench can weaponize burial to purge a rival office or scatter an organized workforce.

## Rejected first ideas

- A distinct regional Techpriest seating and removal panel — refused as the safe continuation explicitly left by the prior Charter ledger.
- An ombuds office for cross-ward petitions — refused as generic administrative furniture already functionally covered by clerks, higher review, and copied petitions.
- A deliberately vacant appellate chair governed by a fixed default schedule — refused because it moved discretion into whoever wrote and interpreted the schedule, creating a hidden regent rather than a new authority form.
- Another three-copy archive challenge — refused because Low Sere's prior Charter pass and the Cinder Concord already own separated-copy pressure.
