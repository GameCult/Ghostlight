# Numen — Prayers in Another Mouth

## Idea

One local Control-descended monotheistic community exchanges prayers by speaking another person's wishes in the first person. A recovered woman's thanksgiving to a neighbouring god tests whether her husband can lend his voice without lending his allegiance.

## What life is like

Menders work among relatives, take satisfaction in exact recitation, voice competitive and embarrassing hopes, and seek a religious encounter through the experience of being heard. Partners can pray alone, choose safe blessings, or entrust difficult words to friends. The practice persists because it is portable, inexpensive, intimate and religiously meaningful; the murmur of simultaneous prayer offers sincere wonder without needing an engineered miracle. The central choice concerns allegiance within a continuing marriage, not departure or expansion.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

People whose desires are routinely corrected gain a valued occasion for being heard accurately. Religious partners gain a shared intimacy that need not require identical wishes. An unwilling speaker can feel pressed into worship; a petitioner can experience refusal as personal rejection. Social expectation can make private prayer look like marital withdrawal. Costs are attention, exposure and sometimes doctrinal discomfort, alongside ordinary time away from work; no new compulsory office or welfare monopoly is added.
