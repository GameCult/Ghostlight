# Ember — The Last Note

## Idea

In a proposed outer neighbourhood of the Luck Crown, residents throw cloth balls at a swinging bowl located by sound, turning fog navigation into a popular contest of timing and accuracy. A noisy spectator variant has injured a passerby by masking a path warning, and an approaching challenge forces neighbours to choose between its crowd, its distinctive skill, and continued use of the carrying route.

## New claims versus established facts

**Established:** The Luck Crown is a fog-hidden city on a desert plateau in the northern coastal mountains; windtraps provide precipitation. Residents cultivate senses other than sight and maintain markers; sound can carry strangely. Family territories include courts, workrooms, gathering sites, shared routes and water structures. Families are not solely blood descent, and no central government owns the city. Luck is constant, unconscious and directly undetectable. Dimna interpretations vary. Leaving the city remains unresolved. The adopted windtrap failure involved neglected maintenance and unproven Luck accusations.

**Proposed:** Hollow Courts and its sheltered hollows; its cloth-sorting use of courts, local repair/trade mix, game, equipment, affordability, scoring, seated play, audience customs, team affiliations and personal relationships; every named role and event in the pressure scenario; an adjoining carrying path, damaged step, obscured warning, injury, alternative route and enclosed workroom. Ordinary pendulum timing, cloth-ball impact and crowd masking are the intended physical mechanisms. Their feasibility is bounded to suitable court dimensions and acoustics, still to be placed; the note establishes no exact throw range or universal ability to locate sound in fog.

**Metaphysical implications:** No new magic, God behaviour, inherited athletic trait, guaranteed fairness or method of identifying Luck. Competition can be affected by existing Luck without players or narration assigning a particular outcome to it. Family victory claims remain situated interpretations. The activity does not shut off Gifts, prove neutrality, turn ordinary play into worship, or resolve city exit.

## What life is like

Residents enjoy precision, suspense, well-timed heckling and the pleasure of knowing someone's habits closely enough to defeat them. Competitive loyalties need not track household disagreements. Quiet players, crowd performers, seated players, meticulous ball makers and uninterested workers share a neighbourhood without sharing an ideal life. The activity persists because common repair materials and temporarily available work courts support repeated local play; visitors here come from neighbouring territories inside the city.

The tonal choice is affectionate sporting rivalry becoming immediate public danger. Admired timing can become vanity. A loud-game enthusiast can demand relocation without repudiating the pleasure that made the sport popular.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Players gain reputation and excitement, callers gain a performance role, and court-side sellers gain trade. The quiet game remains cheap; conspicuous equipment refinement and room-limited matches can nevertheless create distinctions in access. Workers need the same space and nearby paths. The injured porter loses use of a shoulder, while a diversion would impose extra physical effort and exposure on other carriers. Relocation reduces the audience and trading opportunity; changing the format disadvantages a champion whose particular excellence is handling interference. No covert sponsor or hidden atrocity explains the pleasure.

## Rejected first ideas

- Wind-driven cloth glider accuracy contests: could use local materials, but fog would make scoring and retrieval depend on more invented apparatus than the sport earned.
- Racing along fog paths: immediately competes with existing route-custody and hazard stories; throwing from a stationary court lets precision and audience culture carry the novelty.
- A supposedly fair contest that excludes Luck bearers: requires a detection or inherited-certainty claim the sources explicitly deny, and would make adjudication the topic again.
- A feud over proof that a champion's fortune caused an accident: duplicates the adopted Luck-harm work. The proposed danger has an ordinary audible cause while magical attribution remains unsettled.
