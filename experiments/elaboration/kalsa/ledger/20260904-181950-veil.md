# Veil — The Cinder Concord

## Idea

Some Kalsan archives are burned not to destroy them, but to keep any one god from reading them whole through the memories of its bound dependants. The Cinder Concord preserves god-dangerous records as redundant fragments among witnesses attached to rival hierarchies, so restoring a complete account creates a brief moment when every represented god may learn it—and turns disclosure itself into an act of war.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

People whose coerced attachment, refusal, service, or injury was cleaned out of a cult record gain a surviving path to evidence. Rival gods, small cults, civic courts, and dissident offices gain a check against any one hierarchy's complete account; bearers, path keepers, their dependants, and especially deliberately misinformed smoke-bearers absorb seizure, exile, lost welfare, assassination, and the moral cost of defensive lies. Every ember consumes two trained memories, separated routes, repeated recitation, safe lodging, dependant support, replacement labor, and funerary provision; if both bearers die, a cold gap leaves provenance without recoverable words.

## Rejected first ideas

- A Divided City office whose emergency officer is deliberately kept ignorant — refused as a safe procedural extension of existing separated levers and forecast disclosure.
- One more house forecast with a concealed sponsor plan — refused because the Closed Lift Appeal and the earlier Veil pass already own that wound.
- A dead oracle secretly censoring one god's revelations — refused as a singular culprit that would require stronger mind-access mechanics than canon establishes.
- Rival gods agreeing to preserve mutual blind spots — refused because it made divine cooperation do the work that mortal custody, danger, and misinformation should have to carry.
