## What life is like

People acquire favourite sequences, admire precise timing, request repetitions, copy tricks, boast and care who gets credit. Close listeners may love a small, quiet occasion; others enjoy paint, clatter and theatrical misdirection. A relative's delighted request for a repeat can validate an innovation more strongly than an argument. Belonging does not depend on appreciating the family hobby, and changing taste need not become a departure or service dispute.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Performers gain pleasure, reputation and invitations; spectators gain an inexpensive amusement portable between meetings. Wider visual and audible presentation welcomes some people while changing an experience others treasure. Equipment occupies modest carrying space, takes wear and requires an available surface; magical performers also spend effort rather than generating endless motion. Better boards may confer prestige without guaranteeing better performances. The particular harms are dismissal, lost recognition and family possessiveness, not a concealed atrocity. Vesi can be unfair to Oren without her preference for a larger audience becoming fraudulent.
