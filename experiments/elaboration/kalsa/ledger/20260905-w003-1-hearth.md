# Hearth — The Room Kept Ready

## Idea

In a settled Sarxe and mixed-descent neighbourhood, households gradually furnish a spare room for the people they hope to live with. The pleasure of preparing a home lets different relatives mistake their incompatible hopes for a shared future.

## What life is like

People admire a comfortable home and enjoy trying furniture, helping with fittings and eating together. Their aspirations include hosting parents, choosing a companion before illness dictates the arrangement, reducing paid work, raising children, and finally having a personal workbench. Children learn to name the intended movement before carrying furniture and to ask which person's hope an improvement serves. A spare room is both genuinely useful space and an inviting surface for family assumptions.

The disagreement cannot be settled merely by announcing that everyone deserves a choice: there is one room, Mav's earnings matter, and each possible arrangement changes reliable care. Ilket can sincerely want children and family help while declining her father's imagined household.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Residents gain gradual improvements, useful guest/work space, companionship and practical neighbourly help. People with spare space can make generosity conspicuous; poorer households face an unkind comparison. Mav's possible retirement reduces household income. Rul's move would change Azhed's childcare even though she remains nearby. Ilket bears the emotional cost of rejecting a loving expectation. Gifts can be interpreted as larger commitments than their makers intended, without becoming enforceable contracts.
