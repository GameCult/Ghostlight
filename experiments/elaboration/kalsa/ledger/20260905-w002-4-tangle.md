## What life is like

People admire daring, precise description, reliable recognition, and repairs that work. They enjoy telling voyages, disputing names, drawing from descriptions, and watching vessels return. A person can fish locally, build boats, observe from shore, investigate a familiar shoal, or pursue distant headlands while remaining part of the community. Its internal argument is about achievement and allegiance, not whether everyone should leave home.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Fishing households gain new grounds; boatwrights gain work; explorers gain a durable home and financing; neighbouring hamlets gain shared coastal observations. The ruler can turn patronage into prestige and naval expertise. Crews and investors pay for failed voyages, equipment, and injury risk; imported staples and timber make relations with trading towns material rather than ceremonial.

The banner offer is an intelligible bargain, not a concealed trap. It could fund a desired voyage while making independent neighbours unwilling to host it. Fish sellers divide over customers versus supplier influence. Names and public credit can erase existing inhabitants' knowledge without requiring a secret atrocity. Neither faction has blanket authority over Warmhook; crews control their vessels, suppliers their contributions, and neighbouring communities their assistance.
