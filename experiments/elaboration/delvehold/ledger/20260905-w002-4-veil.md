## What life is like

Residents work at fungi growing, salvage sorting, repairs, and provisioning; watching is usually leisure. People admire daring observation, precise drawings, and a boast that survives a rival's visit. Children enjoy spotting substituted objects. An indifferent neighbour can enjoy the performances without spending nights underground. Rell wants prestige from wild discoveries, his sister wants participatory encounters and paid demonstrations, and Vessa wants to know whether a rival has cheated while protecting a discovery of her own. Curiosity need not be altruistic to be worth having.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Watchers gain recognition and companionship; illustrators and demonstrators can sell their work. Local residents have an entertainment made from their own surroundings. Equipment and free time favour some observers, and secretive newcomers may struggle to obtain corroboration. Unannounced feeding can discredit honest observation. Revealed locations attract disturbance and possibly capture; night excursions cost sleep and expose watchers to active-dungeon dangers. The central choice is whether to disclose enough to establish a discovery when disclosure changes the encounter being studied.
