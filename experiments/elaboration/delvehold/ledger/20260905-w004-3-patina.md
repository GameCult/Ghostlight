## What life is like

People make buckles, signs and small pictures for a living; draw their neighbours; look at finished colours in evening light; and disagree about flattering views and faithful ones. Painters and mages need each other's judgment without needing identical skills. Sella likes customers' improvements to reality; Iven values overlooked work and close attention. The stair offers a larger artistic life that both sincerely want, with different ideas about how a public picture should reward its viewers.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Portable work supports makers and gives buyers affordable access relative to a large commission; no general cheapness is asserted. A stair picture gives river workers a place in the town's public art. There is no designated victim or concealed atrocity. Experiments consume glass, metal, mana and paid time; mistakes spoil work. Foregone commissions constrain the mural, while long preparation can become a satisfying livelihood that indefinitely postpones it. Differences in viewing distance and composition create actual artistic choices even with generous funding.
