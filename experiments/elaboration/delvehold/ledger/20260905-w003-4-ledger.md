## What life is like

Work, hot bread and unpacking a returning relative give residents repeated pleasures. Children learn purchasing competence alongside salt work. A good carrier remembers individual requests; a desirable home has enough food and utensils to receive family. Some people choose seasonal wages, some want inherited or newly built pans, and others live across several settlements through trade and marriage. Adra's desired independence is specifically a shared home with her husband. Bek's route contains people he has reason to keep visiting.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Transport ownership commands more bargaining power than access to salt deposits. Pan inheritance also gives some young households an advantage that others must finance through years of work. A cart road could lower freight costs and let Adra enter transport; it would also increase pan owners' margins. Construction consumes labour and grain before it produces income. Existing carriers risk business, while bypassed villages risk regular trade and contact. Bek's alternative fodder stores would support an established livelihood and preserve its unequal entry costs. Neither proposal guarantees cheaper food, and the text supplies no hidden coercive scheme.
