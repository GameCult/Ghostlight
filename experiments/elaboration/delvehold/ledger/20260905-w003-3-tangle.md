## What life is like

Shared blankets, long-distance singing, familiar company during repetitive work and reunions at the fair give the region pleasures worth keeping. People can herd seasonally, weave at home, own flocks, work for wages or run town shops. Admiration for undemanding companionship permits quieter lives. The shared ambition is that children can choose more livelihoods nearby; disagreement concerns how that future will be owned. ## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Clan streets can house relatives cheaply and quickly, sustain seasonal hospitality and convert flock wealth into local employment. Elders gain influence over accommodation; relatives with difficult family relationships have less secure independence. Own-Hearth purchasers gain control over their homes but must save longer, acquire land and finance construction without the same pooled clan wealth. Meadow conversion threatens specific poorer households' winter grazing. Wool dealers gain reliable sales from the league offer; finishing workshops lose access to committed fleece.
