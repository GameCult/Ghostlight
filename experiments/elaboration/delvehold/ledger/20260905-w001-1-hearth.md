## Idea

Bracken Fold is an orcish upland community whose households share work so adults and elders can spend a winter learning something new. Its families must decide how far to support a loved one's growing ambition when further learning may take them away from the people who made it possible.

## What life is like

Seasonal work gathers people into shared repair rooms. Children teach adults as well as being taught, families visit to enjoy imperfect performances, and older people can acquire ambitions after raising children. Belonging grows through relatives, marriage, friendship, and practical help. Some households prize locally useful knowledge; others make room for pleasure, travel, and expertise unavailable at home. The central practice is supported learning across a lifetime, not compulsory performance or a new school administration.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Learners gain time, instruction, and tolerance for failure; children gain teachers who remember being beginners. Shared knowledge and visiting teachers make the practice useful enough to persist alongside its pleasures. Chore substitutes and carers pay in hours, while lessons and travel consume saleable wool income. People with little surplus have fewer turns available. Care can become unfairly concentrated when a successful learner stays away; the proposal leaves this as a family choice to negotiate, without a hidden debt contract or a claim that all households resolve it well.
