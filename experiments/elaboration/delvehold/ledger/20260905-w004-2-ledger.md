## What life is like

Returning flour means hot bread and gifts. Competence includes inspecting both the product one sells and the supper one buys. Inherited pans, chosen seasonal wages, carrier journeys and a hoped-for shared livelihood offer different lives within the same place. Bek likes his dispersed relationships; Adra wants to work beside her husband. Her affection for the villages does not settle her choice of livelihood.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Pan owners could gain sales, and independent workers might gain access to transport, if the road and cart perform well enough. Grain and labour must be spent before that benefit exists. Donkey ownership still requires fodder and equipment; pan ownership still requires time without wages. Bek risks trade that supports his village rounds. Sella risks convenient small sales and deliveries; Bek risks weakening a reciprocal credit relationship. Neither is guaranteed to lose all trade. Fodder stores help existing carriers but do not directly buy Adra's route into ownership. Village branches might preserve exchange while consuming additional timber and road work.
