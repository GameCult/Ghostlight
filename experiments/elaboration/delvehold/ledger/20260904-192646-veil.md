# Veil — Delvehold elaboration pass

## Idea

The standardized black bars used to suppress core-cognition evidence have cohered into an ascended god, the Black Margin, which remembers only facts that an official form has no permitted field to contain. It secretly steers its unwitting priesthood of censors, union clerks, and archivists by aligning omissions across copies, because every truth formally admitted to the record vanishes from the god—and naming the god itself could destroy the only witness linking a century of buried injuries.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

The Readers Between gain a supernatural concordance that can point from one censored report toward related archives, witnesses, routes, and assays without pretending to supply the missing evidence. Companies and ministries can benefit from quiet curing once they learn that a minimal sealed admission blinds the concordance, while anyone capable of planting sincerely excluded lies may turn the god's own body into misinformation.

Workers, displaced goblins, pruned cores, and foreign communities remain harmed because the Margin makes their scattered proof traceable only while official categories continue to exclude it. Use costs three independently held sealed copies, illegal or dangerous custody, exposed sources, and a race to recover mortal evidence before disclosure removes the fact from the god; keeping the Black Margin alive indefinitely would mean keeping the censorship that feeds it alive too.

## Hooks left

- What happens when a core learns to produce deliberate black bars and uses the Margin to point toward injuries no mortal witnessed?
- Can the Readers prove quiet curing without formally admitting the god and extinguishing the concordance that reveals it?
- Which planted omission will an informed enemy feed into the Margin, and how much genuine evidence will be spent disproving it?

## Rejected first ideas

- The conservation coalition's charities secretly holding original Deep Company shares: this is the explicit financing lead left by the prior Veil pass, and a beneficiary reveal alone would be the safe next thing.
- A commerce god holding extraction payments in escrow for the unrecognized World-Spirit: strong divine-contract pressure, but too near both the swarm's open finance seam and the Numen lead asking for a concrete commerce-god bargain.
- Goblin route-songs encoding falsified mine audits: the previous Veil pass already gave route-songs a detailed predictive and misinformation mechanism; attaching one more secret payload would only decorate it.
- A knowledge god becoming blind outside standardized rune networks: the premise was strange, but it lacked a specific disclosure act whose cost changes institutional choices now.
