## What life is like

Pot deliveries connect households; functional pottery provides income, while instruments and music offer distinction and pleasure. People enjoy unpredictable rain rhythms, dry-arcade dancing, attentive listening and remembered performances. Members can be composers, skilled players, listeners or occasional participants without sharing the same religious reading. Heska wants a teaching destination with instrument sales supporting it. Uren shares the musical ambition and has his own contribution, rather than serving as a prohibition attached to hers.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Residents gain an inexpensive shared pleasure using familiar materials, and instrument makers could gain customers and pupils. Musicians spend practice time and sacrifice some saleable pottery to experimentation. No hidden victim is stipulated. The emerging cost is teaching prayers whose invitation may trouble farmers elsewhere and whose growing popularity could influence how worshippers recognize Avar. Neither divine punishment nor weather damage is credited to the song. Shelter and retreat remain practical precautions; devotion does not guarantee safety.

## Hooks left

A pupil's dry-season composition offers a fresh devotional subject that neither established musician has yet judged. These are unresolved choices, not approved resolutions or new universal rules.
