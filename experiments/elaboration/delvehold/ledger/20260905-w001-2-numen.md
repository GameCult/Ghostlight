## What life is like

Warm, skilled, sometimes overattached to the familiar: people fish, grow ordinary food, learn trees individually, entertain guests under out-of-season flowers, and remember relatives through living places. Children practise stopping a spell before exhausting a bud. Excellence includes leaving healthy trees for subsequent learners. Some households give faith its greatest expression in an ordinary shared harvest; others devote their energies to gardening without sharing those observances. Emigration, teaching, earning a living, and raising unfamiliar fruit are independent ambitions.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Families gain pleasure and ways to gather; gardeners gain esteem and possible income from teaching and tree sales. People without inherited land could gain livelihoods from portable practice. Sustained attention and horticultural care limit guesting, so staple meals still come from ordinary agriculture and fishing. Careless forcing damages trees. Long apprenticeships exclude people who cannot leave paid work, while shortened lessons risk selling novices plants they cannot maintain. Neither teacher is hiding an atrocity: they disagree over a genuine teaching problem and have different blind spots about their own families.
