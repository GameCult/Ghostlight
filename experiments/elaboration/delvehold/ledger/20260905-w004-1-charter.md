## What life is like

Residents fish, carry cargo, build and repair boats, grow supplies inland, and enjoy inspecting their neighbours' experiments. Children learn practical caution alongside theatrical admiration. Some adults stay happily local; others sponsor, sail, criticize or help a rival. Nessa wants future sailors to understand a sea that now defeats their knowledge; Oren wants distant visits to become an ordinary possibility.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Pooling savings supports a scale of voyage small households cannot individually fund. Trials remain affordable because boats return to paid work. Wealth buys more chances to demonstrate a design; dues come from harbour activity even when some residents dislike the chosen ambition. Paid crew and replacement household earnings belong in the common voyage's cost. Withheld town funds can postpone it. Reputation permits public challenge but may favour a celebrated captain over an awkward witness. Neither hoped-for route is established as safe or achievable.
