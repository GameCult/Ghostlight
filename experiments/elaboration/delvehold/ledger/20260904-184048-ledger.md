# Ledger — Delvehold elaboration pass

## Idea

The Greathold exports unfinished spells: consortium rune banks impress most of a working into mana, send it through leased spine conduits, and leave a buyer's local terminator to make the effect physical. Under the Last-Stroke Doctrine no good, taxable effect, or product liability exists until that final rune closes, so a town can own crystals and intact pumps yet lose water when a distant supplier null-closes the half-spell—and every Hold along the route can plausibly insist the wealth happened somewhere else.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

- Forge Consortiums benefit by retaining the difficult upstream rune stages while selling cheap terminal hardware, route offices benefit from capacity tolls, and low-duty Holds benefit when firms site one token stage there to move taxable authorship.
- Municipal buyers benefit at first from effects cheaper than locally staffed spellcraft, but their technicians inherit endpoint maintenance without the practice needed to recreate the imported working.
- Independent workshops, displaced utility mages, unsealed residents, and foreign towns are harmed when a remote supplier, route closure, or customs null-stroke can stop water, heat, rail, or refrigeration while local machinery and fuel remain intact.
- The system costs gold or selective conduit material, continuous route capacity, destructive pattern assays, licensed terminators, and redundant local fuel that cannot substitute for missing instructions. Attempts to buy resilience duplicate whole rune chains and increase demand for crystals and deep extraction.

## Hooks left

- If an open working persists, corrects itself, and negotiates route faults across several Holds, is it cargo, a consortium's remote labour, or a new mind entitled to a civic seal?
- When foreign customs closes an imported working into a harmless proof effect, has it inspected a service, manufactured the forbidden good, or destroyed property outside its jurisdiction?
- Are deep creatures striking open-work junctions because those junctions are fragile, or because the dungeon can read an intended pump, ward, or weapon before the last rune makes it physical?

## Rejected first ideas

- First-flow bonds pledging post-failure cistern capacity — too close to the Glasswater lease and Brasswater call-right passes already landed.
- Sleeping claims that revive with abandoned Holds — a strong extension of inherited claims, but still the safe neighbour of the swarm's reserve-debt and counterseal work.
- A commerce god that audits debt by miracle — concrete, but under-grounded here and better left to the Numen mandate's open divine-bargain seam.
