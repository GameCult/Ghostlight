## What life is like

People garden, fish, weave reeds, and make pots for regional exchange. Summer offers household privacy; wet months offer shared warmth, instruction, music, and company. Children learn considerate packing and practical hospitality. Potters can aspire to distant sales or a satisfying set of household bowls. Residents can stay uphill, move seasonally, leave for work, or seek permanent lower homes. Cultural membership is learned through relationships and residence rather than restricted by ancestry.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Shared upper houses make seasonal work, teaching, and sociability practical, while gardening and crafts provide complementary income. Moving requires labour and burdens painful or less mobile bodies. Crowded winters reduce privacy. Permanent raised homes would help people who value continuity and independence; the work costs money, and their absence reduces the shared houses' labour and company. Keeping vacant upper rooms can crowd out incoming residents, while selling makes returning harder. No faction needs to be secretly malicious for this choice to matter.

## Hooks left

An inland pottery offer competes with winter teaching; sisters differ over selling their upper rooms; a newcomer needs the loft used by musicians. Wider unknowns for review are geographical placement, the lake's seasonal hydrology, and whether the proposed property customs suit its eventual jurisdiction. These are proposal admissions, not gaps disguised as source facts.
