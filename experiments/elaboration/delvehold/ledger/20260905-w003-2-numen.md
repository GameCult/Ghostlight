## Idea

A local congregation thanks a celestial love goddess through sweets associated with people who taught them to enjoy those tastes, including relationships that have ended. Worshippers disagree over whether public gratitude honors a real happiness, excuses abandoning difficult love, or keeps an unwanted relationship alive in their neighbours' expectations.

## What life is like

People look forward to tasting what someone else remembers, bringing news of a new affection, and approaching their goddess without disguising a failed relationship. Children learn a short prayer and practical generosity. Adrel wants truthful worship; Tirva wants perseverance to remain admirable. Belis wants to acknowledge his past publicly, while Hadrin wants a present relationship to be received on its own terms. Their different wishes do not map onto species.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

The shrine offers a continuing devotional home to people whose relationships have changed, and confectioners gain modest recurring custom. Public reminiscence can put pressure on a former partner even without a demand for reunion. The offerings cost food and money; attendance costs time. Small purchased portions make participation plausible without inventing universal access or a subsidy system. The durable choice concerns what to say in shared worship when another person's account of the same affection differs.

## Hooks left

Belis has a written speech despite Hadrin's offer of a joint silent offering. Tirva and her husband expect different stories from their anniversary thanksgiving. A confectioner hopes a shared devotion can precede renewed friendship; the friend has requested separate seats. None requires a miracle or a verdict from the goddess to begin.
