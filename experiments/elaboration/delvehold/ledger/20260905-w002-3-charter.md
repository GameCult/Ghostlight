## Idea

The Harrowind Coast is a human maritime culture whose towns choose a generation’s shared voyage by backing rival trial expeditions. Its next choice pits the glory of an unknown crossing against faster journeys that could let distant families see each other more often.

## What life is like

Fishing, smokehouses, farming, boatbuilding, and local travel sustain the region between rare great voyages. Residents enjoy trials as public spectacle, argue about hulls they have physically climbed into, perform expedition stories, and celebrate homecoming. Admired skills include persuasive demonstration, seamanship, daring, and helping a rival succeed while retaining a different aim. Staying near one beloved cove is an ordinary life here. Oren wants quicker family journeys; Nessa wants discovery, and is capable of dismissing his ambition unfairly.

## Who benefits / who is harmed / what it costs

Pooling harbour income lets small ports attempt a voyage beyond a single household’s resources. Trials give practical critics something to inspect and crews something real to learn. Wealthy sponsors can put more boats into the trials; public enthusiasm may follow showmanship. Dues and volunteer effort support an undertaking not everyone wants. A town’s refusal can shrink or prevent the voyage. Public accounts offer sailors a way to challenge captains, but popular songs may entrench a misleading version before a quieter witness is heard. Faster passage is Oren’s hoped-for outcome, not a verified technical capability.
